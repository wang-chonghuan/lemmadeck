## Definitions and Conventions

All radicals are principal real roots. Therefore `sqrt(t) >= 0` for `t >= 0`,
and `sqrt(u^2)=|u|`. The product rule requires nonnegative split factors; the
quotient rule requires a nonnegative numerator and a strictly positive
denominator. Exact decimal literals are exact fractions; mixed numerals are sums.

**Concept block 2.** If `a,b >= 0`, then `r=sqrt(a)sqrt(b)` is nonnegative and
`r^2=ab`. Uniqueness of the nonnegative square root proves `sqrt(ab)=r`. This
does not distribute the radical over addition/subtraction or justify splitting
negative real factors.

**Concept block 9.** Repeated use of the two-factor theorem gives
`sqrt(a1...an)=sqrt(a1)...sqrt(an)` for finitely many nonnegative factors.
Intermediate products are nonnegative, and zero factors are allowed.

**Concept block 11.** If `a>=0,b>0`, then `sqrt(b)>0` and
`r=sqrt(a)/sqrt(b)>=0`, with `r^2=a/b`. Uniqueness proves
`sqrt(a/b)=sqrt(a)/sqrt(b)`. This also supplies the proof requested by the
selected paragraph. A zero denominator is not allowed.

attained. Cancellation does not authorize excluded inputs. This is a
