## 1: Sun to Earth

The intervening space is predominantly near-vacuum, without a continuous
material medium carrying energy from the Sun to Earth. Heat conduction needs
material interactions along its path; convection transports energy with moving
matter. Neither explains the solar energy crossing that intervening space.
Electromagnetic radiation can travel through vacuum and deliver energy that
Earth absorbs. The claim concerns the interplanetary transfer, not the later
conduction/convection that occur after radiation is absorbed at Earth.

## 2: Thermoscope Construction

One side of a glass flask is blackened. The flask contains air; its mouth is
closed by a stopper pierced by a fine-bore glass tube bent at a right angle.
The tube communicates with the flask air and contains a small colored liquid
plug. A scale is attached alongside the tube. A change in the air's thermal
state changes the gas state and displaces the plug, so its position can be
compared. The flask is not shown full of colored liquid. The visible scale
has no temperature numbers or calibration relation. Attaching a scale does
not by itself make an accurate thermometer.

## 3: Complete Demonstration Narrative and Conditions

Describe the textbook apparatus above, not a home experiment. In the historical
demonstration a high-temperature metal block stands beside the blackened flask,
about one metre away. The observed small liquid plug moves to the right:
the flask air receives energy and expands, increasing the apparatus's internal
energy. The metal and flask are not in contact. Across the intervening poorly
conducting air, direct air conduction is insufficient as the main account on
the stated observation timescale.

Putting the flask alongside the source rather than above it reduces the direct
upward hot-air plume contribution. It does not prove convection is exactly zero;
drafts and surrounding air motion must also be controlled. Keep source state,
distance, observation time, apparatus initial state, and orientation comparable.
When comparing blackened and unblackened faces, restore the initial state rather
than treating sequential heating as a controlled comparison.

With these conditions the observed response is evidence of energy delivered
mainly by radiation. It is not a measurement of a specified temperature,
radiant power, or vacuum transmission in this particular air-filled room.
No hot-metal/glass operation or numerical observation is performed or invented.
The capacity to narrate apparatus, observation, controls, competing mechanisms,
and a qualified conclusion is retained as a written-only task.

## 4: Absorbing and Less-Absorbing Surfaces

Under the lesson's relevant material/wavelength/comparison conditions, the
blackened/dark surfaces absorb more incident radiation than the bright,
reflective surfaces; bright metal surfaces typically reflect more and absorb
less. This is not an all-material, all-wavelength consequence of visible color.
Compare illumination spectrum/intensity, exposed area, geometry, and initial
conditions. Greater absorption does not alone prove a greater temperature
increase if losses, work, or phase changes differ. An object absorbing and
losing equal energy can remain at constant temperature.

## 5: Practical Use

Yes. Surface absorption matters when designing for solar heating or limiting
unwanted solar energy input. For the lesson's coatings, silver-white finishes
on balloons/wings can reduce solar absorption; a black coating on a satellite
part may be chosen to increase absorbed solar energy when heating is wanted.
These are purposes of particular coating choices, not a rule for every vehicle
or part. The design also needs outward thermal radiation and other exchanges:
ordinary-temperature bodies radiate too. Do not equate solar-band absorption
with thermal-band emission or infer final temperature from visible color alone.

## g2-1: Building's Three Paths

Through an ordinary solid wall, heat travels within the wall principally by
conduction. The inner wall can transfer heat to contacting air by conduction,
with air circulation distributing it by convection. Interior surfaces can also
exchange radiation, and absorbed radiation can later heat air indirectly.

Warm outside air entering an open window carries internal energy with the
material flow: convection. Sunlight transmitted through window glass delivers
energy by radiation to absorbing room surfaces; those surfaces subsequently
exchange energy with air by conduction and convection. "Window", "wall", and
"glass" are not themselves mechanism names. Different stages and several
mechanisms can coexist; air need not absorb all incoming sunlight directly.

## g2-2: Near an Open Fire

For a person beside a fire/open furnace door, not touching the hot object and
not directly in an impinging hot-gas plume, radiation from the flames/hot
surfaces can be the main cause of the direct baking sensation. Radiation
does not require contact or a carrier-air current. If the person is in a
hot-gas plume or strong wind carries hot gas toward them, convection can be
important. Merely being "near" or "to the side" does not exclude it absolutely.
The source asks for a main mechanism with conditions/reasons, not an exclusive
mechanism in every placement. No new activity near a fire is instructed.

## g2-3: Several Examples With Controls

Examples are reasoning illustrations, not claims of newly performed experiments.

1. The same thermoscope presented blackened-face versus unblackened-face toward
   the same hot radiating source, at the same distance, time, and restored initial
   state: a larger plug displacement with the blackened face supports stronger
   absorption in that source's relevant band.
2. Comparable dark-coated and bright-coated receiving plates under identical
   incident radiation, exposed area, heat capacity, initial state, and comparable
   losses: greater absorbed energy (or a larger initial temperature rise when
   loss/work/phase effects are controlled) can distinguish absorption.
3. In solar-band use, the lesson's silver-white balloon/wing coatings aim to
   reduce absorbed solar energy, while a chosen black satellite-part coating
   aims to collect more. This is an application of a material-specific
   absorption difference, not proof of a universal color rule or an invented
   temperature measurement.

Black versus shiny hot-water-kettle cooling compares outward thermal emission
under its own controls; it cannot alone establish solar-band absorption.
Generating several suitable examples and explaining their controls stays on
the written-answer path, not a choice-recognition substitute.
