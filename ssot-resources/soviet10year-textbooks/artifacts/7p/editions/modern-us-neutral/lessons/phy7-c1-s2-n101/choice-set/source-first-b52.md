## Independent Solution: Written Task 1

For a crystal freezing at unchanged pressure and other relevant conditions,
the freezing interval is the phase-change interval, not the subsequent cooling
of fully solid material. The material continues transferring energy to colder
surroundings. Internal energy includes both molecular thermal kinetic energy
and interaction potential energy. As the liquid becomes crystal, interaction
potential energy decreases; that existing internal-energy contribution is
released during phase change. The molecular average kinetic energy remains
unchanged during freezing, so temperature remains unchanged even though total
internal energy decreases. It is incorrect to infer no heat transfer or no
internal-energy change from an unchanged temperature.

In the lesson's language the released phase-change contribution compensates
the outward energy transfer, preserving average kinetic energy; it is not a
new energy source. Once all liquid has frozen, that phase-change contribution
ends; further outward heat transfer decreases average kinetic energy and
temperature. Pressure constancy and the crystal restriction matter: this is
not a claim about every material or an arbitrary cooling interval.

The essential source distinction is continued energy release versus stable
temperature, reconciled by potential-energy decrease and stable average
kinetic energy. A future choice can test recognition of this causal chain but
cannot prove that the learner independently composed a written explanation.

## Independent Solution: Written Task 2

releases 3.4 x 10^5 J, equal in magnitude to the heat absorbed by the reverse
melting process under the same conditions. Interpreting the written task as
the freezing phase change itself, Q_released = m L = (1 kg)

The direction is release to surroundings, not absorption by the water.
If signed heat into the water is used, it is -3.4 x 10^5 J; the requested
released amount is the positive magnitude 3.4 x 10^5 J.
