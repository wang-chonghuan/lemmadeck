## Source 1: Constant Temperature During Solidification

Task: explain why a crystal solidifying under unchanged pressure and other
conditions maintains its temperature even while energy leaves it.

At the solidification temperature, the liquid state has greater internal
energy than the solid state of the same material. The difference is mainly
intermolecular potential energy. As molecules form the crystal arrangement,
that potential energy decreases and energy is transferred to the surroundings.
During coexistence of liquid and solid, this phase-change energy supplies the
outward heat transfer without a decrease in mean molecular thermal kinetic
energy. Temperature therefore remains constant while total internal energy
decreases. Energy is not created, and a constant temperature does not imply
absence of heat transfer or constant total internal energy.

Once solidification is complete, continued outward heat transfer no longer
draws on ongoing liquid-to-solid conversion. Mean thermal kinetic energy
then decreases and temperature falls. This boundary clarifies the explanation
but is not a separate written task.

Important distinction: temperature follows mean thermal kinetic energy,
not every component of internal energy. The latent-energy reservoir and
outward transfer must both remain present in a correct explanation.

No numerical data or figure is required. The source's unchanged-condition
qualification is sufficient for the textbook model.

Response unit, chosen before options: one complete causal explanation, not
separate clicks for temperature, kinetic energy, potential energy or heat flow.
Choice recognition cannot demonstrate constructing that explanation unaided.

## Source 2: Energy Released by 1 kg Water

Task: determine the positive amount of energy transferred outward when 1 kg
of water becomes ice, considering solidification itself rather than cooling
before or after the phase transition.

The lesson's inspected empirical datum is that 1 kg of ice at 0 degrees C
requires 3.4 x 10^5 J to melt. Expressed per unit mass, the melting datum is
L = 3.4 x 10^5 J/kg. This value is empirical and cannot be calculated from
the qualitative molecular description alone.

For equal masses of the same material under the same pressure and other
conditions, reverse phase changes have equal heat magnitudes and opposite
energy-transfer directions. Thus the water-to-ice process releases energy:

Q_released = m L
           = (1 kg)(3.4 x 10^5 J/kg)
           = 3.4 x 10^5 J
           = 340000 J.

The water's internal-energy change is negative; the requested released-energy
amount is positive. The zero temperature change of the transition does not
make its heat transfer zero. The answer does not require graph 229, a cooling
duration or a specific-heat calculation.

Response unit, chosen before options: one complete result giving energy
direction, magnitude and units. The future stem will provide the melting
datum per unit mass, not the worked freezing answer or the equality rule.
The learner must apply the reverse-process relation and the given mass.
With the original 1 kg mass, the multiplication is necessarily simple;
no ability to handle arbitrary masses or independent written derivations
can be inferred from a choice response.
