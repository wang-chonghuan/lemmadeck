Concrete lawful example in a unit cube:

```text
D=(0,0,0), C=(1,0,0), B=(1,1,0), A=(0,1,0),
D1=(0,0,1), C1=(1,0,1), B1=(1,1,1), A1=(0,1,1).
M=D, N=(1,1,1/2), P=(1,0,1/3).
```

N/P are interior to BB1/CC1. MP has direction(1,0,1/3), not parallel DC; MN has direction(1,1,1/2), not parallel DB. Both stated conditions hold. Yet MP intersects DC at D, and MN intersects DB at D, so F=E=D. The notation `(FE)` does not define a line.

The actual MNP plane still exists uniquely: its three points are noncollinear, as their base projections D/B/C are noncollinear. It differs from ABC and has a unique intersection line through D. Thus the defect is the purported universal two-intersection-point construction and FE naming, not a claim that the plane or actual intersection is nonexistent.
