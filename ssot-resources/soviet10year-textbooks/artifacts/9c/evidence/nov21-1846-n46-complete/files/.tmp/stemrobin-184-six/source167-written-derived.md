## Exercise 377a

$y=x^2(x-2)=x^3-2x^2$.

Domain and range: $\mathbb R$.
Zeros: $x=0$ (double) and $x=2$ (simple).
The origin is the y-intercept and a tangency to the x-axis; the curve crosses
the x-axis at $(2,0)$. It is negative for $x<2$ except at $x=0$,
and positive for $x>2$.

$y'=3x^2-4x=x(3x-4)$.
Increasing on $(-\infty,0)$ and $(4/3,\infty)$.
Decreasing on $(0,4/3)$.
Local maximum: $(0,0)$.
Local minimum: $(4/3,-32/27)$.
Neither is global, since the cubic is unbounded in both directions.

$y''=6x-4$.
Concave down for $x<2/3$, concave up for $x>2/3$.
Inflection point: $(2/3,-16/27)$.
The graph has central symmetry about this inflection point, not about the
origin. Indeed, for $x=t+2/3$, $y+16/27=t^3-(4/3)t$ is odd in $t$.

$y\to-\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$ and $y\to\infty$ as $x\to\infty$.
There are no vertical, horizontal or straight-line oblique asymptotes.

Sketch: mark both intercepts, both extrema and the inflection point. Approach
the origin from below while increasing, turn downward at that tangency, reach
the minimum, rise through $(2,0)$, and continue upward. Match the concavity
change and both infinite tails.

## Exercise 377b

Domain and range: $\mathbb R$.
Zeros: $-\sqrt6$, $0$, $\sqrt6$, all simple crossings.
The y-intercept is $(0,0)$. The function is odd, with central symmetry
about the origin.

$y'=3x^2-6$.

Decreasing on $(-\sqrt2,\sqrt2)$.

Local minimum: $(\sqrt2,-4\sqrt2)$.
Neither is global.

$y''=6x$.
Concave down for $x<0$, concave up for $x>0$.
Inflection point: $(0,0)$.

$y\to-\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$ and $y\to\infty$ as $x\to\infty$.
There are no vertical, horizontal or straight-line oblique asymptotes.

origin to the local minimum, and rising through $(\sqrt6,0)$ to the right tail.
