### `q-positive-improper`

Use the retained \(2.\overline3\) source representation. Integer extraction
leaves remainder 1; the check \(10=3\cdot3+1\) returns remainder 1 each time.
The correct decimal starts recurring at \(N=1\), with period \(p=1\).

- **a: correct.** Equality, post-integer remainder 1, digit 3, repeated remainder
  1, and the explanation that this causes indefinite repetition all hold.
- **b: incorrect.** The initial remainder 1 is true, but a remainder is not the
  next digit. Division of 10 by 3 gives digit 3 and remainder 1, not digit 1.
  The stated decimal equals \(2+1/9=19/9\), not \(7/3=21/9\).
- **c: incorrect.** The equality is true and the next number to divide is indeed
  10 at each step. Calling that 10 the remainder is false: a remainder on
  division by 3 lies in \(\{0,1,2\}\), and it is 1 here. The recurrence
  conclusion is true, but the stated remainder explanation is not.

Frozen complete true set: `["a"]`.

### `q-nonrepeating-prefix`

Use the retained \(0.7\overline6\) representation. The visible initial-state
claim must respect \(230=30\cdot7+20\); the repeating tail obeys
\(200=30\cdot6+20\). Thus recurrence starts at \(N=2\), with period \(p=1\);
the first digit 7 does not recur.

- **a: incorrect.** The first two actual digits are indeed 7 and 6, but the
  remainder after both digits is 20, not the initial 23. The decimal asserted
  here is \(0.\overline{76}=76/99\), differing from \(23/30\) by \(1/990\).
  Returning to 23 and repeating `76` are false for this input.
- **b: correct.** The decimal, first generated remainder 20, next division
  identity, repeated remainder 20, and restriction of the cycle to the 6s
  after the first digit 7 all hold.
- **c: incorrect.** Starting from remainder 20 does give digit 6 and again
  remainder 20, so that local transition is true. However the initial remainder
  is 23, which first gives 7. Starting the 6s at the first decimal place drops
  the prefix and gives \(0.\overline6=2/3=20/30\), not \(23/30\).
- **d: incorrect.** The decimal and first digit 7 are correct. The later digit
  is 6, but the later remainder is 20, not 6. Repetition is real; its stated
  remainder explanation confuses quotient digits with remainders.

Frozen complete true set: `["b"]`.

### `q-negative-long-cycle`

The stem fixes magnitude division and an external minus. Check the retained
six-digit representation against remainders, including the state before any
decimal digit:
\(3\to2\to6\to4\to5\to1\to3\).
The division identities are \(30=7\cdot4+2\), \(20=7\cdot2+6\),
\(60=7\cdot8+4\), \(40=7\cdot5+5\), \(50=7\cdot7+1\),
\(10=7\cdot1+3\). The digit block is `428571`, with \(N=1,p=6\).

  remainder 4 is not 3; its list `4,2,8,5,7,1,4` is not a long-division
  remainder chain. In particular 8 and 7 cannot be remainders modulo 7, and

  The valid repeating digit block does not validate this explanation.
- **b: incorrect.** Six correct leading digits do not make this finite value
  equal to the source. The displayed zero-tail value is \(-428571/1000000\),

  remainder is 3, not 0. The general rule that a zero remainder forces a zero
  tail is true, but its claimed occurrence here is false.
- **c: correct.** The signed decimal is correct. Every listed remainder,
  including the initial and returned 3, agrees with the six division
  identities. The repeated state uniquely restarts the six-digit block,
  justifying indefinite repetition.

Frozen complete true set: `["c"]`.
