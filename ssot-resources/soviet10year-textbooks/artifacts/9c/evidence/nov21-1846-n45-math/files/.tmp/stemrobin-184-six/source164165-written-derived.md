For a fixed x where u and v are differentiable, let w=uv. Write
u(x+h)=u(x)+Delta u and v(x+h)=v(x)+Delta v. For h!=0,

Delta w/h = v(x) Delta u/h + u(x) Delta v/h + Delta u Delta v/h.

Differentiability gives Delta u/h -> u'(x), Delta v/h -> v'(x).
It also gives continuity: Delta u ->0. Thus the cross term tends to
0*v'(x)=0, and w'(x)=v(x)u'(x)+u(x)v'(x).
No division by u, v, u', v' is used; zero values are allowed.

Example1:
((x+3)(x+15))'=(x+15)+(x+3)=2x+18, for all real x.
Expansion x^2+18x+45 gives the same derivative.

A constant k has k'=0, including k=0:
(k f(x))'=0*f(x)+k f'(x)=k f'(x) wherever f is differentiable.
Example2: (x/15-3)'=1/15 for all real x.
