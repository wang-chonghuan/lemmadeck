1. Source p0064#4. Acceleration is the change of velocity vector per unit time.
   For uniform acceleration, a = (v - v0) / t is constant. Its direction is the
   direction of the velocity change, not necessarily that of motion. Knowing
   initial velocity and acceleration permits calculation of later velocity:
   v = v0 + at. A definition and its reason are required; ungraded.

2. Source p0064#5. Acceleration describes the rate and direction of change of
   velocity. A larger magnitude means a faster velocity change. The velocity
   vector may change in magnitude or direction. In general varying motion the
   finite-interval ratio is average acceleration, while acceleration at a given
   instant is instantaneous acceleration. Do not claim that every finite
   interval ratio gives a constant instantaneous value. Ungraded explanation.

3. Source p0064#6. In straight-line motion, speed increases when acceleration
   has the same direction as the current velocity and decreases when they are
   opposite. The sign comparison refers to projections on the same fixed axis.
   After reaching zero speed, continued fixed acceleration may reverse motion;
   a negative acceleration alone does not always mean slowing. Ungraded.

4. Source p0064#7. Uniformly accelerated motion has equal changes of velocity
   vector in any equal time intervals, equivalently constant acceleration.
   Equal changes in speed alone are not the general vector definition.
   Ungraded definition.

5. Source p0064#8. Initially at rest, a = 1.5 m/s^2. Target 54 km/h =
   54 / 3.6 = 15 m/s. t = (15 - 0) / 1.5 = 10 s. Auto numeric10,
   unit seconds. Display both conversion and calculation.

6. Source p0064#9. The printed initial speed is 36 km/s = 36000 m/s.
   Choose the positive axis in the initial direction: v0 = 36000 m/s,
   v = 0, t = 4 s, a = (0 - 36000) / 4 = -9000 m/s^2.
   Asked "how large": acceleration magnitude is 9000 m/s^2 = 9 km/s^2.
   Auto numeric9000 if the requested unit is m/s^2. Display the signed
   projection and positive magnitude; do not change the original source unit.

7. Source p0064#10. v0 = 15 m/s, v = 25 m/s, a = 1.6 m/s^2.
   t = (25 - 15) / 1.6 = 6.25 s. Auto numeric6.25, unit seconds.

8. Source p0065#1. The body stops after 0.5 h = 1800 s with acceleration
   magnitude10 m/s^2. Over the stated interval it slows uniformly. Choose
