## Mathematical domain

Use the ordinary Euclidean plane, closed rays containing their origins, and
proper rays: [AB) requires A != B. An origin may coincide with another ray's
origin; that does not invalidate either proper ray.

Write R(A,u) = {A + t*u : t >= 0}, u != 0. Two rays have the same direction
iff their direction vectors are positive scalar multiples. They have opposite
directions iff the vectors are negative scalar multiples. Nonparallel supports
give neither relation. This algebraic description proves the source statements;
it does not replace or redefine the source's geometric definitions.

Containment in the collinear definition is non-strict: equality counts.
For distinct parallel supports, their origins A and C are necessarily distinct.
The line AC meets each ray only at its origin, and each ray's non-origin part
lies in one open half-plane of AC. Alternatively use closed half-planes with
their common boundary. Do not literally put the whole closed ray in an open
half-plane.

Ordinary polygons below are simple, nondegenerate, with genuine vertices and no
redundant collinear side subdivisions. A triangle is noncollinear. Degenerate
zero-vector "rays" and artificially subdivided polygons are outside this domain;
equal origins, coincident supports and touching ray intersections are not.
