### ray-symmetry-laws

Write the opposite rays as R(A,d) and R(B,-d), d nonzero.
At C=(A+B)/2, H_C(A+t*d)=B-t*d for all t>=0, so the entire rays correspond.

regardless of where P is.

### polygon-possibilities

Mode: multiple. Complete true set: **B, E**.

In the view's strictly convex domain a side lies on a supporting line.
For any unoriented direction only the two extreme parallel supporting lines
can contain sides, with at most one side on each because straight-angle
vertices are excluded. Hence parallel pairs use disjoint pairs of sides
and their number is at most floor(n/2).

All counts up to the bound are attained. The hash-bound source-first record
already supplies independent strict convex coordinate witnesses for:
4 sides with 1/2 pairs, 5 sides with 1/2, and 6 sides with 1/2/3.
For clarity, those witnesses are respectively:

- 4/1: (0,0),(4,0),(3,2),(1,2); 4/2: (0,0),(4,0),(4,2),(0,2).
- 5/1: (0,0),(4,0),(5,2),(3,4),(1,4);
  5/2: (0,0),(4,0),(4,2),(3,3),(0,3).
- 6/1: (0,0),(4,0),(6,1),(5,3),(3,5),(1,5);
  6/2: (0,0),(5,0),(5,2),(4,4),(2,5),(0,5);
  6/3: (0,0),(3,0),(4,2),(3,4),(0,4),(-1,2).

Their repeated slopes are horizontal only for the 1-pair witnesses,
horizontal/vertical for the 2-pair witnesses, and 0,2,-2 for 6/3.
Zero is attained for each n by (x,x^2), x the first n values of
1,2,4,8,16,32, joined in convex cyclic order. Adjacent and closing slopes
are different binary sums of two powers of two.
Together these prove the complete possible sets, not just upper bounds.

For E, a half-turn of a simple polygon without straight-angle vertices
permutes the corners. A fixed corner would have to be the center, with its
two incident directions exchanged by negation, making them collinear.
That is excluded. All corner orbits therefore have two members, so n is
even. The argument permits concave simple polygons and needs no regularity
or unsupported assertion that a concave polygon's center is interior.
