## Definitions Actually Used

These are from the allowed original prose, not modern conventions:

1. s1: A circumference is the planar set `C(O,r)={X:|OX|=r}`,
   and a circle is the closed disk `D(O,r)={X:|OX|<=r}`, with **r>0**
   in both definitions. An arbitrary point set is a geometric figure;
   the prose also admits the empty set.
2. s3: For same-kind quantities `a=x e`, `b=y e`, the ratio is `x/y`
   **only when b is nonzero**. A usable measurement unit is nonzero.
3. s4: Distance is positive for distinct points, zero for a repeated point,
   symmetric, and satisfies the triangle inequality. The unique-line
   axiom applies to two distinct points. The book expressly says phrases
   such as "two points" normally mean distinct points.
4. s5: X is **between** A and B precisely when all three are different and
   `|AX|+|XB|=|AB|`. Euclidean collinearity and triangle equality are
   related by its separately admitted proposition 7. That proposition
   must not be transferred to the road metric in 157.
5. s6-s7: Segments include endpoints; rays include their initial point.
   Coordinate distances are absolute differences; changing unit rescales
   both coordinates and numerical distances.
6. s8: A simple open polyline has at least two nonzero segments, adjacent
   segments are noncollinear, and nonadjacent segments have no common point.
   A simple closed polyline closes a simple open chain and has these
   conditions in cyclic order, hence no branches or interior crossings.
   Length is the sum of segment lengths. Its final note says subsequent
   uses of "polyline" mean a **simple** open or closed polyline.
7. s9: Planar figures lie in a plane; later figures are in the same plane
   by default. Earlier prose allows spatial polylines, but does not turn
   the word "square" into a cube.
8. s10: A region R has both properties: every two points can be joined
   within R by a segment or a polyline; for each P in R some **positive**
   radius closed disk centered at P is wholly in R. Boundaries are excluded.
   The written two universally quantified properties do not expressly add
   nonemptiness; the counterexamples below do not rely on an empty-set
   convention.
9. s11: A polygon is a planar simple closed polyline **together with its
   interior region**. Its edges and vertices come from that polyline.
   A diagonal joins nonadjacent vertices, even if it is outside a concave
   polygon. Convex means including the segment between every pair of
   member points; singleton and empty figures are expressly convex.
10. s12: A half-plane **includes its bounding line**. An angle is two
    rays with a common initial point **plus one of the two bounded regions**;
    thus the same noncollinear rays determine one convex angle and one
    nonconvex/reflex angle. Opposite rays define straight angles.
    An arc is circumference intersected with its central angle; its
    endpoints are included. Two distinct circle points determine two
    proper arcs; no full circumference is silently called a proper arc.
11. s13: Distinct circumferences are tangent when they have exactly one
    common point, crossing when they have exactly two, disjoint when they
    have none. With positive radii and center distance d, external tangency
    is `d=r1+r2`; internal tangency is `d=|r1-r2|>0`; crossing is
    `|r1-r2|<d<r1+r2`. Coincident equal-radius circumferences are not
    one-point tangencies. Concentric means same center, not equal radius.

For explicit construction witnesses below, ordinary planar coordinates are
used to specify the drawing without producing a rendered asset. For a point
`(x,y)`, the distance to `(u,v)` is `sqrt((x-u)^2+(y-v)^2)`. Nontrivial
uses of convexity, tangency, path length or separation are justified below;
no external answer convention is taken as a premise.

### 155

> 155. 证明：两个量的比与度量单位的选取无关. 也就是说，如果 $x_1$ 和 $x_2$ 是以 $e_1$ 为度量单位时 $a$ 和 $b$ 两个量的量数，$y_1$ 和 $y_2$ 是以 $e_2$ 为度量单位时两个量的量数，那么 $x_1:x_2=y_1:y_2$.

### 156

> 156. 点 $A$ 在圆 $(O,r)$ 内，距离 $|OA|$ 等于 $a$. 证明：圆 $(A,r-a)$在圆 $(O,r)$ 内.

If 0<a<r, take X on the ray from O through A with OX=r. Then AX=r-a:

claim that every point is strictly inside the outer disk is false.

is false although subseteq holds. If "A in the circle" admits a=r,
the displayed radius r-a=0 is outside the book's positive-radius circle
definition. Its inequality set would be the singleton {A}, contained in
the larger disk, but calling it a circle requires an explicit extension.
