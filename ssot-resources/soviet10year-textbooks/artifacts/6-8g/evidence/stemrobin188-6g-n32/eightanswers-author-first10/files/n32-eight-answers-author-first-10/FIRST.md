437: the second side of an acute angle is oblique to the supporting line of the
first side; corollary2 gives intersection with any perpendicular to that line.
Distinguish supporting lines from actual rays. If perpendicular to the first

only yields a supporting-line intersection, not an intersection of the actual ray.
State this scope explicitly rather than pretending an extension point is on a ray.

439: a central symmetry permutes the distinct component lines, preserving
directions. A finite parallel-line family has a center exactly when its distinct
normal offsets are reflection-symmetric about the center's normal offset.
1) distinct parallel pair: infinitely many centers on their equidistant middle
parallel; coincident pair: infinitely many points on the one line.
2) for three distinct lines with a parallel pair and a nonparallel third:
zero centers. The third must map to itself, so the center lies on it; the parallel
pair must swap, putting the center on their middle line. But reflecting the third
about this point leaves its direction and could indeed leave it invariant.
This initial zero conclusion requires checking: a line through a candidate center
is invariant under half-turn, regardless of angle. Thus there IS one center:
the intersection of the third line and the middle parallel. Retain this reasoning
correction here before candidate creation.
For three distinct parallel lines: infinitely many centers on the middle line
only if the middle is equidistant from the two outer lines; otherwise zero.
Coincident input lines reduce the union to one or two distinct lines: one line
has infinitely many centers; two parallel lines have infinitely many; two
intersecting lines have exactly one center at their intersection.
3) two distinct intersecting lines: exactly one center at their intersection;
two parallel lines: infinitely many along the middle parallel; coincidence:
infinitely many points on the one line. A center outside the intersection cannot
fix both nonparallel components, and a half-turn cannot swap their directions.
