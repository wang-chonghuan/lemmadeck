### 493: Definitions and all three comparisons

Obtuse: a triangle with one interior angle greater than 90 degrees.
Right: a triangle with one interior angle equal to 90 degrees.
Acute: a triangle with all three interior angles less than 90 degrees.
Triangle sum ensures there cannot be two right/obtuse interior angles.

Write the compared angle as A; B+C=180-A.
- A=B+C implies 2A=180, so A=90: right triangle.
- A>B+C implies 2A>180, so A>90: obtuse triangle.
- A<B+C implies only A<90. The whole triangle is NOT uniquely classified.
  (60,60,60), (30,90,60), and (30,120,30), with the first entry A,
  satisfy the comparison and are respectively acute, right, and obtuse.
  All three triangle types are possible.

### 505: Actual alternative proofs, and the precise comparison boundary

1. **Triangle, alternate-interior route:** Through A, rather than C, draw a
   line parallel to BC. Select its two opposite rays so that the half-plane
   containing B,C is divided by AB and AC. The outer sectors equal B and C
   by alternate-interior angles; the middle sector is A. Their sum is the
   straight angle, 180. This uses no side extension through C, no upper
   4/5/6 construction, and no vertical-angle substitution, so it is genuinely
   different from the actual proof exposed in 491.

   **Further distinct triangle route, half-turn copies:** Rotate ABC by 180
   degrees about the midpoint of AC, sending A to C and B to P; rotate it
   about the midpoint of BC, sending B to C and A to Q. These are congruent
   copies, so angle PCA=A and angle BCQ=B. In directed point arithmetic,
   P=A+C-B and Q=B+C-A, hence P+Q=2C: CP and CQ are opposite rays.
   The rays CA and CB lie between them in the half-plane containing A,B:
   with C as origin, det(A-B,A)=det(A-B,B)=det(A,B), with nonzero common
   sign. Thus A+angle ACB+B is a straight angle. This constructs congruent
   pieces rather than appealing to the theorem being proved.

2. **Convex polygon, interior-point fan:** Choose O strictly inside the
   polygon and join it to ALL n vertices. Convexity keeps each segment inside;
   these n triangles tile the polygon. Their total angle sum is n*180.
   Their angles at O make a full turn of 360. The remaining triangle angles
   split, and exactly exhaust, the polygon's interior angles at the vertices.
   Therefore S=n*180-360=(n-2)*180=2d(n-2).
   This is a complete proof with a separate central full-turn subtraction,
   not the unsupported assertion that there are n-2 triangles in this fan.

   **Second polygon route, induction:** The triangle is the base case.
   For n>=4, diagonal V_1 V_3 splits a convex n-gon into triangle
   V_1 V_2 V_3 and a convex (n-1)-gon. The angles at V_1,V_3 split
   additively; every other interior angle is unchanged. By induction
   S_n=180+180*((n-1)-2)=180*(n-2). This uses one ear removal at a time.
   Neither polygon proof invokes theorem 49, avoiding possible circular use
   of an exterior-sum theorem derived from the desired interior-sum theorem.
