## Domains And Universal Quantifiers

A relation R on M is a subset of M x M. Its properties require:

- Reflexivity: for every x in M, xRx.
- Symmetry: for every x,y in M, xRy implies yRx.
- Transitivity: for every x,y,z in M, (xRy and yRz) implies xRz.

Variables need not be distinct; in particular x=z is allowed. One
counterexample disproves a universal property. A failed property does not say
that every instance fails. Equivalence means all three properties on the same
stated domain. Symmetry is not antisymmetry.

For the table and line exercises use all Euclidean straight lines in a fixed
plane. Lines are geometric sets, not finite displayed strokes. For congruence
use geometric figures in the same Euclidean setting and an isometry taking one
to another; segment congruence specializes to equality of lengths. For order
use all real numbers with the ordinary < and <= relations. For polygons use
bounded plane polygons having well-defined ordinary area. For segment symmetry
use all ordinary, nondegenerate, unordered-endpoint segments in the plane with
one fixed point O. Including degenerate segments does not change the general
verdicts, though the singleton {O} is another fixed object.

## Table Reconstruction

Equality-inclusive parallelism means same direction, including coincident
lines: a P b iff dir(a)=dir(b), where direction is an unoriented 1-dimensional
subspace. It is reflexive, symmetric and transitive because equality of
directions has these properties. Equivalently, in the plane the lines either
coincide or have no common point. This reading is mathematically required by
the supplied table's +,+,+ and the stated parallel equivalence classes; it is
not a claim that an earlier source-page definition has been inspected.

Strict disjoint parallelism means a Q b iff a and b are coplanar and
a intersection b is empty. It is NOT the table's equivalence relation.
Exercise 449 isolates this difference. Adding equality is a substantive
definition choice and must not be silently hidden.

Perpendicularity is not reflexive because a line's direction is not
perpendicular to itself. It is symmetric. It is not transitive: take a:y=0,
b:x=0 and c:y=1. Then a is perpendicular to b and b to c, but a is not
perpendicular to c.

Congruence is reflexive by the identity isometry, symmetric by the inverse
isometry and transitive by composition. These facts do not depend on the
unresolved identity of the printed congruence glyph.

For <: x<x is false; 0<1 but not 1<0 disproves symmetry; x<y<z implies x<z.
For <=: x<=x; 0<=1 but not 1<=0 disproves symmetry; x<=y<=z implies x<=z.

## Partition Theorem

A partition of M consists of nonempty blocks whose union is M and which are
pairwise disjoint. Define xRy iff x and y belong to the same block.
Coverage gives reflexivity; reversing membership gives symmetry; if x,y share
one block and y,z share another, their shared element y forces those blocks
to be the same, proving transitivity.

Conversely, for an equivalence relation define [x]={y in M : xRy}.
Reflexivity ensures x in [x], so the classes cover M and are nonempty.
If t belongs to both [x] and [z], xRt and zRt; symmetry gives tRz, then
transitivity gives xRz. For any u in [x], symmetry gives zRx and transitivity
gives zRu, so [x] is contained in [z]; reversing gives equality.
Thus two classes are either equal or disjoint.

Finally xRy iff [x]=[y]: xRy gives equality by the preceding reasoning;
if classes coincide, y in [y]=[x] gives xRy. The partition is determined by R.
Parallel classes comprise every line of one direction, including the member
line itself. Figure 213 supplies only finite samples of two such classes.

## Independent Exercise Solutions

### 446: Congruence On Segments

Domain: all Euclidean segments. Let ell(s) denote a segment's length.
sRt iff ell(s)=ell(t). Every segment has its own length; equality is symmetric;
ell(s)=ell(t) and ell(t)=ell(u) imply ell(s)=ell(u).
Answer: reflexive YES, symmetric YES, transitive YES; an equivalence relation.
Classes consist of segments of each fixed length. Position and orientation do
not matter. This does not identify the source congruence glyph.

### 447(1): Equal Polygon Area

Domain: polygons with well-defined ordinary area. L R L1 iff A(L)=A(L1).
Area equality is reflexive, symmetric and transitive. Answer: YES, YES, YES;
an equivalence relation, with classes by area.
Equal area does NOT imply congruence: rectangles of side lengths 1,4 and 2,2
both have area 4 but are not congruent.

### 447(2): Segment Images Under One Fixed Central Symmetry

Fix O and write H(p)=2O-p. For a segment x=[A,B],
H(x)=[H(A),H(B)]. The relation is xRy iff y=H(x), not merely equal length.
Since H(H(p))=2O-(2O-p)=p, H is involutive on points and segments.

Reflexivity fails on the full domain: at O=(0,0), x=[(1,0),(2,0)] is sent to
y=[(-1,0),(-2,0)], which is not x. A nondegenerate segment is fixed precisely
when its midpoint is O, so some instances xRx do hold; not all do.

Symmetry holds: y=H(x) implies H(y)=H(H(x))=x, hence yRx.

Transitivity fails: for the same x and y, xRy and yRx hold. Set z=x.
The required xRz is xRx, which is false. Equivalently, y=H(x) and z=H(y)
force z=x, not z=H(x). Involution is not transitivity.

Answer: reflexive NO, symmetric YES, transitive NO; not an equivalence relation.
On a domain consisting only of fixed segments the verdict would instead be
YES, YES, YES, but that is not the source's unrestricted segment question.
The different relation y=x OR y=H(x) is an orbit equivalence with one- or
two-element classes; do not replace the stated relation by it without an
explicitly identified change of question.

### 447(3): Lines Having "One Common Point"

The wording does not, within these supplied inputs, settle whether "one"
means at least one or exactly one. Retain both readings:

1. At least one: aIb iff there exists p with p in a and p in b.
   On all plane lines, this IS reflexive: a line has points in common with
   itself. It is symmetric. It is not transitive: a:y=0, b:x=0, c:y=1 have
   aIb and bIc, but a and c have no common point.
   Answer: YES, YES, NO; not an equivalence relation.
2. Exactly one: aJb iff there exists p in both such that every common point
   q equals p. This is NOT reflexive: a line coincides with itself and has
   infinitely many common points. It is symmetric. It is not transitive:
   the same three distinct lines give two unique intersections and no
   intersection for a,c.
   Answer: NO, YES, NO; not an equivalence relation.

The non-equivalence conclusion is stable, but the reflexivity answer changes.
Do not silently treat coincident lines as forbidden while claiming to test a
relation on all lines. A relation's property quantifiers still include a=b.
If a modern prompt explicitly selects one reading, its answer must match it.

### 448: Several Equivalence Relations

Valid examples, each with a stated domain and all three properties:

- Congruence of plane segments: equality of lengths, as proved in 446.
- Equal area of plane polygons: equality of areas, as proved in 447(1).
- Equality-inclusive parallelism of all plane lines: equality of direction.
- Congruence modulo 3 on all integers: mRn iff 3 divides m-n.
  Reflexivity uses 3 dividing 0; symmetry uses negation; transitivity uses
  (m-n)+(n-k)=m-k. The three classes are the residue classes 0,1,2 modulo 3.

An answer may give other valid examples, but must define the relation and its
domain. Strict-disjoint parallelism and the fixed-central-image relation above
are not valid examples on their stated full domains.

### 449: The Student's Strict-Disjoint Parallel Definition

Domain: all lines in a fixed Euclidean plane. Relation Q requires no common
point; equality is excluded by that requirement.

Reflexivity fails for every line a, since a intersection a=a is nonempty.
Symmetry holds because emptiness of intersection is symmetric.
Transitivity fails: take a:y=0, b:y=1, c=a. Then aQb and bQc, but not aQc.
This repeated-variable example is legitimate and sufficient. Distinct-variable
chains do not rescue a universal transitivity claim.

Answer: reflexive NO, symmetric YES, transitive NO; not an equivalence relation.
On a singleton or other restricted line domain with no strict-parallel pair,
transitivity would hold vacuously, but reflexivity still fails. The actual full
plane-line domain contains such pairs, giving the failure above.

The equality-inclusive repair "same direction, including coincidence" is
reflexive, symmetric and transitive and matches the table and partition example.
It is a proposed explanatory contrast, not an edit to the raw source.
