## Every new369 option, solved before the key

A: INCORRECT. Its opposite and coincident components agree with FIRST, but
the noncollinear union of both complete line bisectors is too large.
For rightward/upward rays, C=(-1,-1) on a bisector has both feet behind O,
and C=(1,-1) on the other bisector has one foot behind O. Both have positive
line-distance radius but cannot be tangent to both actual rays.

B: CORRECT. For noncollinear unit directions u,v, C=t(u+v), t>0, is exactly
the smaller-sector bisector ray minus O. Opposite rays require a nonzero
perpendicular center and share contact O, giving the punctured perpendicular
line. Coincident rays require C=a u+b n with a>=0 and b!=0, precisely S\L.
This checks all three components and all relevant boundary inclusions.

C: INCORRECT. Opposite horizontal rays admit C=(0,1), radius 1, with tangent
contact O on both rays. Thus the second component is not empty. The other
two components do not repair that false complete triple.

D: INCORRECT. For coincident rightward rays, C=(0,1), radius 1, has its foot
at O and is valid. It lies on the boundary of S, outside L, but is absent
from the interior of S minus L. The third component wrongly requires
strictly forward rather than permitted endpoint contact.
