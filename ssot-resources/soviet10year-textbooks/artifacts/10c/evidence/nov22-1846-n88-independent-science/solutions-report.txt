EXERCISE 231
Requirement: PROVE both inverse derivatives using the inverse-function formula;
retain the raw reference to subsection 112 without reading an unrelated section.
For y=A(x)=arctan x in (-pi/2,pi/2), tan y=x, tan'(y)=sec^2 y=1+x^2>0.
The inverse derivative formula gives A'(x)=1/tan'(y)=1/(1+x^2).
For z=C(x)=arccot x in (0,pi), cot z=x and
cot'(z)=-csc^2 z=-(1+cot^2 z)=-(1+x^2)!=0.
Thus C'(x)=1/cot'(z)=-1/(1+x^2).
Both proofs apply to EVERY real x; 1+x^2>0 never vanishes.
The inverse output branches avoid the poles of their original functions.
Final results: arctan'(x)=1/(1+x^2), arccot'(x)=-1/(1+x^2).

EXERCISE 232

Then z=pi/2-y lies strictly in (0,pi) and

Alternative using exercise 231: derivative of the sum is zero on connected R;
at x=0 the sum is 0+pi/2, so it is constantly pi/2.
This includes x=0 and negative x without reciprocal shortcuts or branch shifts.
