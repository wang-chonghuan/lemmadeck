CONVENTIONS
All unmarked angles are radians. Explicit degree marks retain degree units.
Z denotes all integers, R all real numbers.
ctg/cot denotes cos(t)/sin(t), NOT a reciprocal defined at sin(t)=0.
arcctg/arccot is the inverse of cot restricted to the OPEN interval (0,pi).
arctg/arctan uses the principal OPEN interval (-pi/2,pi/2).
Analytic interval notation here uses conventional parentheses/brackets for
reasoning only; raw French-style interval characters are not edited.

1. COTANGENT DOMAIN AND REGULARITY
D(cot) = R \ {k*pi : k in Z}.
At every k*pi, sin(t)=0 and cos(t)=(-1)^k != 0, so cot is undefined.
On its open, disconnected domain cot is continuous and differentiable (indeed
smooth); this does not assert continuity or a finite value at any removed point.
In degree units its excluded arguments are 180*k degrees.

2. DERIVATIVE AND BRANCH MONOTONICITY
On D(cot), the quotient rule gives
cot'(t) = [(-sin t)(sin t) - (cos t)(cos t)] / sin^2 t
        = -(sin^2 t + cos^2 t)/sin^2 t
        = -1/sin^2 t < 0.
Each connected branch B_k=(k*pi,(k+1)*pi) is therefore strictly decreasing:
for u<v in that SAME branch, the mean value theorem gives cot(v)-cot(u)<0.
The negative derivative does NOT make cot globally decreasing on D(cot).
Counterexample across a pole: 3*pi/4 < 5*pi/4 but
cot(3*pi/4)=-1 < 1=cot(5*pi/4).
Thus even global nonincreasing behavior fails. No comparison may cross a pole
using the branch-decrease theorem. Equal values one period apart also rule out
global strict decrease.
For an angle variable measured numerically in degrees, the derivative includes
the positive factor pi/180: -(pi/180)/sin^2(pi*d/180); its sign is still negative.

3. BRANCH LIMITS, RANGE AND ASYMPTOTES
At a pole k*pi, right limit cot(t)=+infinity, left limit cot(t)=-infinity.
On B_k the left-end limit is +infinity and right-end limit is -infinity.
Continuity and strict decrease show each branch maps bijectively onto R.
Therefore E(cot)=R; cot is unbounded above and below.
Vertical asymptotes are t=k*pi. Removed points are not included endpoints and
are never assigned cotangent values.
These graph properties are analytic deductions, not inspected figure evidence.

4. ODDNESS, PERIOD AND ZEROS
For t in D(cot), cot(-t)=cos(t)/[-sin(t)]=-cot(t); the domain is symmetric.
cot(t+pi)=(-cos t)/(-sin t)=cot(t), with domain preserved.
To prove minimal positive period: a period T sends the zero at pi/2 to a zero.
All zeros solve cos t=0 with sin t != 0, hence t=pi/2+k*pi.
Consequently T=k*pi, so the smallest positive period is pi.
Zeros: t=pi/2+k*pi, all included in the domain.
Positive on (k*pi,k*pi+pi/2).
Negative on (k*pi+pi/2,(k+1)*pi).
The raw negative interval (k*pi-pi/2,k*pi) is the same negative-branch family
after an integer reindexing, not a sign error.

5. INVERSE DOMAIN, RANGE, IDENTITIES AND DERIVATIVE
Restrict cot to B_0=(0,pi), not its entire periodic domain.
Its inverse C(a)=arcctg(a) exists uniquely for every a in R.
D(C)=R; E(C)=(0,pi); neither 0 nor pi is attained for finite input.
C is continuous, differentiable, and strictly decreasing on its entire
connected domain R.
From cot(C(a))=a, differentiate:
[-1/sin^2 C(a)]*C'(a)=1.
Since 1+cot^2 C(a)=1/sin^2 C(a)=1+a^2,
C'(a)=-sin^2 C(a)=-1/(1+a^2), for EVERY real a.
As a tends to +infinity, C(a) tends to 0 from above.
As a tends to -infinity, C(a) tends to pi from below.
Inverse graph horizontal asymptotes are y=0 and y=pi.
C(0)=pi/2; C(-a)=pi-C(a), so this branch of arccot is not odd.

Let A(a)=arctan(a) in (-pi/2,pi/2).
The angle pi/2-A(a) belongs to (0,pi) and has cotangent a.
By uniqueness, C(a)=pi/2-A(a) for every a in R.
Thus A(a)+C(a)=pi/2, including a=0 and all negative inputs.
A'(a)=1/(1+a^2), from the inverse derivative of tan.
Reciprocal shortcuts need branches:
C(a)=arctan(1/a) for a>0;
C(0)=pi/2;
C(a)=pi+arctan(1/a) for a<0.
Do not use arctan(1/a) at zero or on negative inputs without this adjustment.
cot(C(a))=a for every real a.
If t in (k*pi,(k+1)*pi), then C(cot t)=t-k*pi, not generally t.

6. EQUATIONS AND ALL-SOLUTION FAMILIES
For finite real a, cot(t)=a iff
t=C(a)+k*pi, k in Z.
Existence and completeness follow from the bijection on (0,pi) and period pi.
Because C(a) lies strictly between 0 and pi, all listed solutions avoid poles.
For cot(b*x+c)=a with b != 0:
domain x != (k*pi-c)/b;
solutions x=(C(a)-c+k*pi)/b, k in Z.
Endpoints where b*x+c=k*pi are undefined, not extra solutions.
Lesson example 2 gives x=pi/4+k*pi for a=1.
Lesson example 3 gives x=pi/6+k*pi for a=sqrt(3).
Both are complete families, not merely principal roots.
