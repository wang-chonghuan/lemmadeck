## Source1: Measuring Friction

Original task: "怎样测量摩擦力？"

One workable method is to attach a force gauge to a block and pull it horizontally along a horizontal surface in steady straight-line motion. Read the gauge during the steady motion. With no other significant horizontal forces, the pull and opposing friction balance: zero horizontal resultant, so their magnitudes are equal. The gauge reading then gives the sliding friction magnitude. Concept3 supplies the measurement equivalence for uniform motion.

Necessary conditions: gauge zero/calibration appropriate; horizontal pull; actual uniform straight-line motion rather than starting, speeding up or slowing down; no additional horizontal hand force; stable contact and load. Friction acts opposite relative sliding, while the pull acts forward. A nonzero gauge reading is compatible with uniform motion: it is the resultant, not each force, that is zero.

The initial maximum reading needed to start motion is not interchangeable with the subsequent steady sliding reading. Nor does every instantaneous pull reading equal friction during acceleration. The seed supplies no numerical reading, friction coefficient, arbitrary static-friction protocol or maximum-static-friction data.

## Source2: Dependence On Force Pressing The Surfaces Together

Original task: "怎样证明摩擦力与将物体压向接触面的力有关？"

First measure the steady horizontal sliding reading for a block. Then add a weight on top, or press vertically downward on it, and measure again by the same method. Keep the same block bottom, surface, materials, contact orientation, horizontal pull and comparable steady sliding speed. Record and compare the readings: a greater force pressing the contact surfaces together gives a larger friction reading in the stated course experiment. Repetition strengthens the comparison. Concept4 explicitly authorizes added weight or downward pressing and the qualitative increase.

Changing the pressing force is the experimental variable; changing the roughness/material or pulling at a new angle would confound it. Added weight should not change the block's contacting face. A pressing hand must not add an unaccounted horizontal push. On a horizontal support without vertical acceleration or an upward pull component, the support load increases with the added downward force.

This proves the stated qualitative dependence in controlled comparisons. It does not establish an exact proportionality or doubled friction merely from a doubled mass, and it supplies no measured numbers. Both the comparative procedure and why the force-gauge readings represent friction are parts of the written task, not just the sentence "more pressing force, more friction."

## Source3: Sliding Versus Rolling At Equal Load

Original task: "怎样用实验证明，载荷相同时滑动摩擦力大于滚动摩擦力？"

Compare force-gauge readings during steady horizontal motion in a sliding arrangement and in a rolling arrangement under the same total supported load. A possible textual arrangement is to pull a block directly on a surface, then let the comparable load travel on freely turning cylindrical rollers and pull it steadily again. The rolling setup must actually roll rather than skid or have its rollers locked. Keep load, supporting surface, comparable contact materials, pull direction and steady-motion conditions controlled as far as the two motion modes allow. Account for roller/support weights if they materially alter the supported load.

Both readings represent the corresponding resistance through force balance, not transient starting peaks. The observed rolling reading is smaller than the sliding reading, consistent with concept6. No diagram geometry, roller count, numerical reading or exact ratio is supplied by the seed.

"Same load" is essential: a lighter rolling arrangement versus a heavier sliding arrangement cannot isolate motion mode. Likewise, switching to a smoother material at the same time would weaken the claimed comparison. This is the course's controlled experimental comparison, not a universal theorem about every bearing, surface, deformable ground or arbitrary rolling machine. Rolling resistance need not be zero; idealized absence of slipping does not itself prove a zero gauge reading.
