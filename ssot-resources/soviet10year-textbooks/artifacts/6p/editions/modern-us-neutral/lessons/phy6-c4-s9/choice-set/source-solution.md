## Source 1: What Is Earth's Atmosphere?

The atmosphere is the layer of air surrounding Earth. “空气海洋” is the textbook's
analogy; it is not liquid seawater, the Earth itself, or only clouds. “大气” names
the material air layer; “大气压强” names a physical effect of that layer and is
not the definition of the atmosphere. The historical approximate height account
in concept0 is attributed to this textbook and is unnecessary to answer this
definition task; no new universal height or sharp boundary is inferred.

Required production: state the definition independently in words.
Choice recognition can preserve the distinction but cannot demonstrate that
independent formulation.

## Source 2: What Produces Atmospheric Pressure?

In this lesson's static-air description, gravity acts on the air; upper air
compresses lower air. The air layer consequently exerts pressure on Earth's
surface and objects there. Air transmits pressure in all directions, rather
than restricting its action to the downward direction of gravity. The weight
of an object standing on the ground is not what creates the surrounding
atmosphere's pressure; wind is not required. This explanation stays with the
selected source model and does not substitute an unselected microscopic theory.

Required production: give the gravitational/compression cause, not merely repeat
“大气存在” or list an unrelated pressure example. Independent connected causal
explanation is a capacity not proved by selecting a statement.

## Source 3: Describe Experiments Showing Atmospheric Pressure

Two complete examples are available in the allowed lesson and actual images.
The source task is open: these examples do not exhaust possible acceptable
experiments, and it does not supply a fixed number or measured data.

1. Fig140: use a glass tube with a tightly fitting piston and immerse its open
   lower end in an open reservoir. Raise the piston while maintaining the seal.
   Water rises inside the tube. Raising the piston reduces pressure below it;
   outside atmospheric pressure acts on the exposed reservoir water and drives
   water into the lower-pressure region. It is not a vacuum pulling water by a
   special attractive force, nor necessarily a piston mechanically gripping
   and carrying the water. The seal and submerged intake matter. In a real
   apparatus the available pressure difference must support the water column;
   the drawing supplies no limitless height or calibrated pressure.
2. Fig141: seal the cylinder around its one tube, remove some air using the
   pump, keep the switch closed to retain low pressure, immerse the lower tube
   end in reservoir water, then open the switch. Water enters through that
   continuous tube and jets into the vessel. Atmospheric pressure on the
   outside water exceeds the retained internal pressure sufficiently for this
   demonstration; the pump need not keep running during the displayed jet.
   Closing the switch blocks that route. Venting the vessel first removes the
   pressure difference, so merely opening this immersed tube does not produce
   the same upward jet. Gravity alone does not propel water upward.

These comparisons separate setup, observation and causal inference; the image
shows a demonstrated state but cannot by itself prove the pressure explanation.
No extra measurements, pressure values, numerical scale or additional figure
is supplied. The headless images do not imply that the source experiment was
