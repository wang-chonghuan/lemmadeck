### s20-grip-traction-judgments

Grip measurements use the hand's compression of the instrument and its
calibrated response. Traction measurements require the instrument to receive
the pull in the coupling/load path, with appropriate range and calibration.
Same pointer angle across different instruments does not fix force equality.
The supplied dial position is not an N conversion or a speed reading.

| Option | Independent truth | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | true | With the same normally functioning grip instrument in its working range, its calibrated readings compare the squeeze forces in the two trials. It does not establish maximum capability or compare different calibrations. |
| B | false | The statement claims that the position ALONE permits a less-than-3-N conclusion. No N unit or multiplier is provided for the 2..3 dial sector, so that inference is invalid. The actual traction might be smaller or larger than 3 N under different calibrations; it is the claimed entailment, not an invented unknown numeric value, that is false. |
| C | false | Each instrument has its own force-to-deflection mechanism and calibration. Equal angle need not mean equal force; a grip instrument and a large-force traction instrument may map that angle to different magnitudes. The universal word '一定' is not justified. |
| D | true | A suitable-range traction dynamometer connected in the actual traction coupling receives the measured pulling load; reading its calibrated indication is the correct use. Mere co-motion without loading would not suffice. |
| E | false | Force indication is not a speed calibration. Co-motion does not change the measured quantity into velocity; different speeds can occur with the same pulling force under different load/resistance conditions. |
