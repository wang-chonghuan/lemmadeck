## Source1: Two Necessary Conditions

For one specified constant force to do nonzero work, that force must act and the
body must have displacement with a nonzero component along that force. Force
and any displacement alone are not sufficient: a perpendicular force does
zero work. Parallel same-direction displacement gives positive work, opposite
displacement negative work. This answer describes a specific force, not all
forces or the resultant at once.

Production loss in recognition: no independently written definition or
explanation of the direction qualification.

## Source2: Two Quantitative Factors

Under the stated constant, same-direction model, work is the product of force
magnitude and displacement along that force. At fixed displacement, work is
proportional to force; at fixed force, it is proportional to displacement.
Doubling either factor while holding the other fixed doubles the work.
Neither inverse proportionality nor an unqualified force-only dependence
follows. This is not a universal nonnegative formula for opposing forces.

Production loss: writing the complete two-factor law and explaining both
controlled comparisons independently.

## Source3: Unit

The SI work unit used here is the joule. It is not the newton (force), watt
(power), kilogram (mass) or metre (length). Kilojoule is a1000-joule multiple,
not a second definition of a unit force acting through a unit distance.

Production loss: unaided recall/name production.

## Source4: Joule Definition

One joule is the work of a1-newton constant force moving its object1 metre
along the force: 1 J =1 N·m. A1-metre displacement perpendicular to that force
does not realize this definition; the force then does zero work.
Force value, displacement value and direction are all needed.

Sources3/4 form one coherent name-and-operational-definition task. Recognition
can cover that relationship but cannot prove independently writing it.

## Source g2-1: All Five Situations and Their Conditions

1. Boy climbing a tree: his body rises. Gravity on the body is opposite this
   upward displacement and therefore does negative work in the whole-body
   translation model. Muscular activity can perform work on moving body parts;
   one must not label all forces positive merely because climbing is effortful.
   This model is not a detailed analysis of work at static hand/tree contacts.
2. Girl playing piano: when a finger pushes a moving key along its downward
   displacement, the finger's force on that key does positive work. The text
   does not supply a force record for every moment of performance.
3. Person standing with a sack of grain: if the sack remains stationary, the
   support force and gravity on the sack do no translational mechanical work,
   despite muscular effort/energy use by the person.
4. Worker supporting/raising a door with the shoulder: “撑起” alone is not a
   complete movement record. If the shoulder raises the door along the support
   force, the force can do positive work; if the door is only held still, zero.
   Force alone cannot establish positive work. Do not silently replace the
   open source wording with a fixed moving or fixed stationary case.
5. Water pressing a container wall: identify the pressured object and whether
   its force application region moves. A rigid stationary wall has zero work
   by that pressure; a moving/deforming wall requires its displacement and
   directional relationship. Pressure alone does not determine the work.

Meaningful recognition must account for all five source objects and for the
unknown door/wall conditions. Lost capability: independently choosing the
force/object and writing a qualified explanation for each scenario.

## Source g2-3: Ideal Ice-Sliding Ball

Under smooth horizontal motion with ice friction/air resistance absent,
there is no ongoing horizontal driving force required to maintain the motion.
Gravity and vertical support remain present but are perpendicular to horizontal
displacement, so each does zero work. It is incorrect to infer no forces at all,
or positive work merely because the ball continues to move.
Zero resultant does not in general imply every individual force does zero work;
this particular ideal case has zero work by its two perpendicular forces.

Its application of the nonzero-work criterion is coherent with source1.
Production loss: an unaided force inventory and inertia/direction explanation.

## Source g2-4: Crane

Uniform vertical lifting without other resistance makes the upward lifting
force equal in magnitude to weight. Define local gravitational field strength
g in N/kg. Then F=mg and lifting-force work is mgh, positive:

2500 kg × g ×12 m = (30000 kg·m)g.

The raw/modern written prompt and selected definitions do not provide a
numerical g. A unique joule number therefore needs an explicit extra local
field convention; it is not silently supplied by the seed. Conditional on
g=9.8 N/kg, the result is294000 J; under a different declared g it changes.
Gravity itself does the opposite signed work; resultant work is zero in this
uniform-motion model. Neither is the requested lifting-force work.
