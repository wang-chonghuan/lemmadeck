## Source 1: What Is the Weight of a Body?

The weight of a body is the force that the body, because of Earth's attraction, exerts on its support or suspension.

The body is the agent exerting this force. Its recipient is the support or suspension, not the body itself. A body resting on a horizontal table presses the table; a body hanging from a vertical suspension pulls the suspension at their contact. Both are included in the definition, so limiting weight to only suspended bodies or only table-supported bodies would omit a stated case.

Weight is a force, not a mass. If a numerical weight were given, a force unit rather than a mass unit would be required. These tasks contain no mass, gravitational-field value or force magnitude to calculate; none should be manufactured.

## Source 2: Weight Versus the Gravity Acting on the Body

The gravitational force on the body is Earth's force on that body. Earth is its exerting agent; the body is its recipient.

The body's weight, under the supplied definition, is the body's force on a support or suspension. The body is its exerting agent; the support or suspension is its recipient.

They must therefore be distinguished by their exerting and receiving bodies, not merely by names or the numerical values of their magnitudes. Calling gravity and weight two names for Earth's same force on the body erases the recipient distinction. Calling the support's force on the body its weight reverses the weight's agent and recipient; it describes the supporting force instead.

For the depicted right-hand body hanging at rest from a vertical suspension, gravity on the body is vertically downward. The body pulls its suspension downward at the contact, so its weight is also downward there. The upward force by the suspension on the body is not the body's weight. The stated rest/vertical-suspension conditions make the directions definite; do not apply this direction statement to arbitrary tilted or accelerating apparatus.

The source asks for a conceptual distinction, not a force diagram, a numerical result or a proof of a general equality between the two force magnitudes. Magnitude equality is not needed to solve or grade the selected tasks.
