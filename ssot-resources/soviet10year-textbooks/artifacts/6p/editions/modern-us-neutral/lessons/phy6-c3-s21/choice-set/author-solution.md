## 1: Examples For All Three Force Attributes

The task is to generate examples establishing the separate relevance of
magnitude, direction and application point, not just list their names.

Independent examples:

- Magnitude: hold one end of the same elastic spring fixed. In the same
  static, elastic range and direction, a larger pulling force extends it
  farther. The range and apparatus conditions matter; no universal assertion
  that every effect is monotone for every object is intended.
- Direction: at the same position on a door, opposite perpendicular pushes
  can tend to open or close it, keeping other conditions comparable. Equal
  magnitudes do not make opposite directions the same action.
- Application point: for the same door and equal pushes perpendicular to
  its plane in the same sense, a push near the handle has a larger turning
  tendency than one near the hinge. A force applied through the hinge has
  zero lever arm for this axis. A complete example must specify comparable
  directions and distinguish turning from translation.

The source permits other valid examples. No particular apparatus is the
unique answer. Supplying examples for recognition loses autonomous invention,
selection of controlled comparisons and unaided causal explanation.

## 2: Why Force Is A Vector

A force must specify both magnitude and direction. Two 10 N forces directed
oppositely are not the same force vector even though their magnitudes agree.
Newton is a unit of force magnitude, not the reason for vector status.
Application point affects what the force does to a body, but it does not
replace the magnitude-and-direction vector definition or become a required
third component of the abstract vector.

## 3: Representing A Force In A Diagram

A directed line segment encodes a force:

1. Its beginning is the point where the force is applied to the receiving body.
2. Its head points in the direction of the force on that body.
3. Its total start-to-tip length is proportional to magnitude under a declared
   common scale.

For scale S newtons per centimetre and force magnitude F newtons, the drawn
length is F/S centimetres. Different scales can validly represent the same
force; equal force lengths mean equal magnitudes only under the same scale.
Thickness, darkness and arrowhead size are not the magnitude encoding.

In fig-68 the applied point is A at the body/shaft junction and the direction
is up/right. Its numerical force cannot be recovered because that particular
figure gives neither a magnitude nor a calibration. Pixel length cannot
silently turn it into a ruler.

The complete source task includes actually drawing and labeling such a
representation. Selecting a correct description or drawing plan cannot prove
that the learner can position and construct it unaided.

## 4: Difference Between Drawing Gravity And Weight

Use the lesson's distinction illustrated in fig-69: gravity F acts on the
body; weight P is the body's action on its support or suspension.

For a bag resting on a horizontal support, draw the downward gravity vector
from an interior representative point of the bag, and draw the downward
weight vector from the contact point ON the support. These are forces on
different recipients even when their magnitudes agree in a stationary case.
An upward force of the support on the bag is not the weight P in this usage.
The gravitational distributed force is idealized as an interior resultant;
no exact centre-of-mass location is measurable from a qualitative sack.

The original task also covers suspension: there the weight starts where the
body acts on the suspension and points along that action, not generically
upward because the suspension pulls upward on the body. Recognition of the
approved supported-bag example alone does not demonstrate making a new
gravity/weight drawing, or transfer to every suspended configuration.

## g2-1: 400 N Weight And Horizontal 50 N Strike

Choose positive scales S_w and S_h in N/cm independently, or use one common
scale S. Valid lengths are 400/S_w cm and 50/S_h cm.

For example, a common scale 100 N/cm gives lengths 4 cm and 0.5 cm.
Separate scales 200 N/cm and 10 N/cm give 2 cm and 5 cm. Both are legitimate;
the drawing must declare which scale applies. Comparing raw lengths across
different scales cannot compare force magnitudes.

For a body on horizontal support, the 400 N weight vector begins on the
support contact and points down. For the strike, begin at the point on the
ball receiving contact and point horizontally in the force direction.
The written source supplies horizontal direction but NOT left versus right;
either horizontal orientation is acceptable if chosen consistently. It also
does not specify the support/suspension geometry of the 400 N object.
An eventual concrete recognition question must explicitly state any chosen
support geometry and horizontal sense as newly authored conditions.

Choosing suitable scales without candidates, using a ruler, placing origins,
directions and labels, and actually making both drawings are part of the
source production task. Recognition of complete scale-and-geometry plans
retains reasoning about them but cannot certify that production.

## g2-2: Tractor Traction At A Specified Scale

## g2-3: Three-Tonne Brick Crate On A Stationary Cart

3 metric tonnes = 3000 kg. In the stationary supported case, magnitudes of
gravity on the crate and weight on the cart are both 3000g N when g is
expressed in N/kg. Draw gravity down from an interior resultant point of
the CRATE and weight down from the crate/cart contact ON THE CART.
Equal magnitudes do not make them forces on the same recipient.

The selected source prose supplies no numerical g. The general source
solution therefore remains 3000g N. An eventual numerical choice question
must visibly specify g rather than borrowing a solved example. With the
explicit authored condition g=9.8 N/kg, both magnitudes are 29400 N,
or 29.4 kN. These are exact under that chosen parameter; no rounding to
30000 N is required.

At common scale 10 kN/cm, draw both lengths 2.94 cm. At common scale
5 kN/cm, draw both lengths 5.88 cm. Using separate declared scales is also
valid and can give unequal drawn lengths for equal forces; the source
does not require one unique scale or length. A complete plan must state
its scale for both vectors and preserve their different application objects.

The source's autonomous scale choice, spatial drawing, physical ruler
