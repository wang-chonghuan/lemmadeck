## 1 / g1

With the same amount of gas and approximately unchanged temperature, compressing
reduces available volume and increases molecular number per unit volume.
Molecules strike unit wall areas more frequently, so average normal force
per unit area and pressure increase. Expanding under the same conditions
reverses this: density/collision frequency decrease and pressure falls.
The flexible membrane's outward/inward deformation indicates internal
pressure relative to the surrounding air; it is not an absolute gauge.

The statement is conditioned on unchanged gas amount and comparable
temperature. Simultaneous heating/cooling or gas leakage can change the result.
No exact pressure-volume law or factor is requested. Full explanation required;
ungraded, not a one-word or fixed-number answer.

## 2 / g1

For a fixed amount of gas in the same fixed container volume, heating increases
pressure, cooling decreases it. Higher temperature means larger typical
molecular speeds; molecules strike walls more often and transfer more
momentum per collision on average. The resulting average force per unit
area increases. Do not say collisions merely become numerous while individual
impacts stay unchanged, nor that cold gas stops moving.

Without the unchanged-amount/unchanged-volume comparison, temperature alone
does not determine which pressure is larger. The raw lesson supplies those
conditions even though the short question does not repeat them. No numerical
ratio or absolute-temperature conversion is needed. Retain ungraded with both
answer and causal explanation.

## 3 / g1

Compression permits substantial gas to be stored in a relatively small space,
but increases the pressure on the container walls. A dedicated strong cylinder
is required to withstand that pressure and contain the gas. The classroom
argument is about containment strength; do not infer an exact safe rating
or prescribe operating/filling procedures from the schematic image.

Not every gas has the same pressure, and mass is not a force unit. The question
asks why, not what numerical material specification to use. Full explanation,
ungraded.

## 4 / g1

In the source's idealized comparison, one cylinder of gas is connected to an
equal-volume evacuated/initially much lower-pressure cylinder, keeping the
combined gas amount fixed. Gas spreads into the second volume; after a
common equilibrium is reached at the same comparison temperature, the
available volume is larger and pressure is lower than in the initially
filled high-pressure cylinder.

The question requests a qualitative change, not a mandatory numerical half.
Half follows only for equal accessible rigid volumes, initially evacuated
second vessel, unchanged final temperature and the ideal-gas approximation.
If "empty" means containing ambient air, the amount is not the original
one-cylinder amount; with initial pressures p1>p2 and equal-volume
same-temperature idealized vessels, final pressure is between the two.
Do not silently write p1/2 as a universal consequence, invent an initial
pressure, or assert pressure disappears.

Future comparison must follow final modern wording and any explicit
definition of empty/equilibrium. Retain ungraded with complete reasoning.
