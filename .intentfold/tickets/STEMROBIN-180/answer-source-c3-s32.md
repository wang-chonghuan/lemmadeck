## Question1 / g1 / 1

To demonstrate gas pressure on container walls, use the lesson's tied,
partly filled balloon in a bell chamber. Initially it is not spherical.
Remove air surrounding the balloon while retaining the balloon's enclosed
gas: it swells, showing that the internal gas acts outward on its wall.

Molecular reasoning: gas molecules continually strike the wall. Reducing
the external gas reduces its opposing effect while the balloon retains
its gas, so an outward pressure difference expands the flexible membrane.
At the eventual static balance, the pressure difference is balanced by
membrane elasticity; it is not necessary for inside/outside gas pressures
alone to be equal. The balloon's expansion also changes its internal state,
so do not claim its pressure stayed numerically fixed throughout.
The source provides no measured pressures or required calculated result.
Complete observation plus explanation is required; retain ungraded.
