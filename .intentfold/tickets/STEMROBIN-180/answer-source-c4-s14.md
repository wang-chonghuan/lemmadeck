1. The instrument is a manometer / pressure gauge (压强计), used here to measure
   pressures above or below atmospheric pressure. A barometer is the distinct
   instrument used to measure atmospheric pressure.
2. An open liquid manometer has liquid in a U tube, one branch exposed to the
   atmosphere and the other connected to the measured gas or diaphragm box.
   Gas pressure acting on the connected branch is compared with atmospheric
   pressure. Higher connected pressure depresses its surface and raises the
   open branch; lower connected pressure reverses that difference. At
   equilibrium the liquid-column difference balances the pressure difference,
   p_measured-p_atm = rho*g*(z_open-z_connected). Same levels mean equality,
   not absolute zero pressure. Greater membrane compression and deeper liquid
   immersion increase the difference. At the same depth, turning the membrane
   changes orientation but not pressure. These require explanation, not just a
   guessed reading; use ungraded with a complete standard explanation.
3. The metal manometer has curved hollow metal tube1, one end closed and the
   other connected through stopcock4 to the measured vessel. Increased internal
   pressure partly straightens it; closed-end motion goes through lever5 and
   gear3 to pointer2. Elastic return at lower pressure moves the pointer toward
   zero. Do not swap this tube mechanism with the aneroid's evacuated box.
