## 1 / g1 / 问题

Examples: broad building foundation, wide tires, tracked vehicles, skis or
snowshoes. For comparable normal supporting force, larger actual supporting
area gives smaller average pressure p=F/S, reducing sinking into yielding
ground. Give reasonable examples and the load/area mechanism, not a bare
list or the claim every large object has low pressure. Open examples; ungraded.

## 2 / g1 / 问题

Wide agricultural-machine wheel rims increase effective support/contact
area under comparable load, reducing average pressure on the soil and the
tendency to sink. This is the pressure argument, not automatic greater
friction or lower total weight. Actual contact area/material and load matter;
no numerical factor can be read from the source. Ungraded explanation.

## 3 / g1 / 问题

Sharp cutting edges and points concentrate the applied normal force on a
very small contact area. Thus even moderate force can produce large local
pressure and help cut/pierce. Sharpening changes area, not creates force from
nothing; no universal infinite pressure or given material strength.
Ungraded causal explanation.

## g2-2 / 练习19 / 2

Adding ballast increases the downward load transmitted through the rake's
teeth while their relevant contact area is approximately unchanged. Greater
force per area increases pressure, helping teeth enter hard soil.
Not merely greater contact area or changed soil density. No specified ballast
mass, tooth count or pressure value; ungraded.

## g2-3 / 练习19 / 3

In the elementary comparison, standing normally loads two soles; during
running support can be carried on one sole or a smaller part, so equal-load
area reasoning predicts larger pressure in that supporting phase.
Running also has dynamic ground reaction: landing/push-off force can exceed
weight, increasing pressure further. Flight phase has no floor contact, hence
zero contact pressure; ground reaction and area vary during a stride.

Therefore do not state pressure is always greater at every running instant,
nor assign a universal exactly-double factor. State the comparable
contact/support-phase condition and pressure mechanism. Raw short question
has no measured force/time/contact-area data. Ungraded full explanation.

## g3-1 / 作业 / 1

Measure learner mass m and each sole's actual supporting outline on grid
paper. Count complete enclosed squares n and boundary-intersected squares k;
estimate one-sole support area A=(n+k/2)*(1/4) cm^2. Forefoot and heel count;
noncontact arch gap does not. Convert square centimetres to square metres
by multiplying10^-4 before using pascals. Weight F=mg with the course's stated
choice of g, carrying units consistently.

For equal soles and ordinary two-foot standing, area approximately2A and
p_stand=mg/(2A). A simplified slow single-support comparison uses
p_walk approximately mg/A; for actual walking, normal force N(t) and contact
area A_contact(t) vary, so p(t)=N(t)/A_contact(t). No universal exact factor
without the simplified conditions. Record unequal soles separately if needed.

No learner mass, outline-square count or actual support-force data is given.
Do not invent numbers from the illustration or claim a choice response
validates measuring/drawing. Ungraded answer must retain the entire procedure,
unit conversion, both standing/walking comparisons and approximation limits.

## g3-2 / 作业 / 2

Measure brick length l, width b, height h; express lengths in metres.
Compute V=lbh in m^3. Select the appropriate brick/material density rho
from the source table (kg/m^3), then mass m=rho V and weight F=rho lbh g
in newtons. Use actual table entry/material, not a guessed universal density
or weight in kilograms.

Compute face areas lb, lh, bh. Flat on lb: p=F/(lb)=rho h g.
On lh: p=F/(lh)=rho b g. Upright on bh: p=F/(bh)=rho l g.
All pressures are pascals. For ordinary l>=b>=h, flat pressure is smallest
and upright pressure largest; otherwise compare measured face areas.
Draw the same rectangular prism in all three orientations, marking the
supporting face and corresponding vertical dimension. No new measured
dimensions, numeric density or final pressures can be inferred here.

Ungraded personal measurement, density lookup, complete calculation and
orientation-drawing task; do not reduce to a bare ranking or pretend the
illustration supplies the learner's measurements.
