1. Treat bottle, tube, valve and tied balloon as the same closed system:
air transfers internally, so total mass and true gravitational force are
unchanged. Inflation increases external displaced-air volume. With static
ambient air, the left assembly's buoyancy increases by
rho_air*g*delta_V, so its apparent downward pan load decreases. The left
pan rises/right pan descends after equilibrium is re-established, assuming
the right weights and outside conditions are unchanged. It is not a loss
of gas mass or proof that compressed air has no weight. Preserve the request
to perform and explain the classroom experiment, but do not fabricate an
observed result, a successfully executed procedure or measured tilt.

2. In ambient air the large low-density sealed bulb experiences greater
buoyancy than compact weights of comparable apparent load. Initially:
g*m_b - rho_air*g*V_b = g*m_w - rho_air*g*V_w.
For V_b > V_w this gives m_b > m_w. Evacuation removes most buoyancy from
both sides, not their weight. The bulb side's apparent load increases more,
so it descends and the compact-weight side rises. The actual displayed
left-low state agrees. No gas escape from the sealed bulb, new mass, unequal
gravity or force from the absence of air is required. Residual gas merely
reduces the ideal-vacuum change; no quantitative pressure is given.
