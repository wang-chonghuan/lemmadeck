## S15

g1/1: Reducing pressure beneath the rising sealed piston permits atmospheric
pressure on the supply water to push water into the inlet. The piston does not
pull a water column by an intrinsic suction force.

g1/2: Cylinder, close-fitting movable piston, upward-opening inlet and piston
valves. Upstroke: bottom inlet opens, piston valve closes, water beneath rises
and previous water above the piston is delivered through the spout. Downstroke:
bottom valve closes and the piston valve opens, passing water above the piston.
Repeated strokes deliver water. Source160(a) depicts the upstroke; do not infer
a second operating state from its artwork.

g1/3: For a surface suction pump the ideal limiting height satisfies
p_atm = rho_water * g * H with nearly zero pressure at the column top, neglecting
losses/vapour pressure. The textbook benchmark is approximately 10.3 m under
its atmospheric/density assumptions, not a universal actual practical lift.
Real usable suction height is lower and pressure-dependent.

g2/g2-1: Mercury under standard atmosphere: approximately 760 mm = 0.76 m.
Its greater density means a much shorter supported column. Use the supplied
standard-atmosphere/rho/g precision, not a fabricated experiment.

g2/g2-2: H = p_atm/(rho_alcohol*g); using rho_alcohol = 800 kg/m3 and the
textbook standard pressure gives approximately 12.9 m. Equivalently use
H_alcohol/H_water = rho_water/rho_alcohol. If the final task does not give or
authorize this density, state the dependence instead of inventing a numeric
premise. This is an ideal pressure-limited comparison, not safe pumping advice.

g2/g2-3: Source160(b) shows a downward rod arrow, closed lower suction valve2,
open delivery valve3 leading to air chamber4, a discharge riser and handle5.
The force-pump upstroke draws water through2 while3 prevents backflow; downstroke
closes2 and forces water through3 into the chamber. Compressed chamber air
stores pressure and continues driving discharge between strokes, smoothing
flow. It does not remove the atmospheric limit on lifting water from below the
pump. Such a surface suction arrangement cannot draw from more than the ideal
10.3m benchmark at the stated conditions; pressure discharge height and suction
lift are different quantities. Preserve both diagrams' different mechanisms.
