1. A specified force must act and the object must have displacement with
a nonzero component along that force. Mere force plus arbitrary movement
does not guarantee nonzero work: perpendicular force has zero work.

2. In the chapter's constant-force, parallel, same-direction model,
A=F*s, proportional to force and distance. Direction matters in the general
case; opposite-direction force does negative work. Distinguish work by one
force from total work by all forces.

3. SI work unit is joule, J;1kJ=1000J.

4.1J is work by a constant1N force when displacement1m is in that force's

5. g2-1: climbing involves upward force and displacement; piano playing
involves force and motion of keys/hammers/strings, not just a moving person.
Standing still with a sack has no mechanical work on the stationary sack
by the support force, although the person expends metabolic energy.
Shouldering a door UP does work during lifting; merely holding it fixed
does not. Water pressure on a fixed container wall does no work because
the wall has no displacement; a moving wall would change that premise.
Preserve these phase distinctions instead of assigning a context-free key.
