1. Floating ship: its watertight hull encloses sufficient volume including
air; static buoyancy rho_liquid*g*V_sub equals total ship/cargo weight.
Steel density alone is not the whole ship's average density. No bottom
support or flooding is assumed. A real ship can sink if loading/flooding
exceeds its feasible displaced volume; buoyancy is not unbounded.

2. Draft is the depth of the submerged hull, measured vertically below the
water surface to the relevant bottom of the hull; not total ship height.

3. In the source's convention the marked load line identifies maximum
permitted draft. Do not equate its color to a universal modern certification
standard or confuse actual waterline with the maximum marker.

4. The source defines displacement here as displaced-water WEIGHT at the
permitted load line, equal to ship-plus-cargo weight. Its unit is force.
Modern common usage may use displaced mass, but do not silently change
this source definition to volume, cargo mass or tonne mass.

5. Same loaded ship, static equilibrium, negligible air, denser seawater:
rho_sea*V_sea=rho_river*V_river, so V_sea<V_river and draft decreases for
the ordinary upright hull as its waterline falls along the hull. This is
conditional on seawater being denser and no loading/attitude change; no
numerical densities or draft decrease are supplied.

7. Complete q1 hydrometer assignment: the actual172 shows two water
containers, upright floating weighted glass bodies, differently positioned
bulbs/stems, many unnumbered scale marks, a water-surface1000 reference on
each, and Cyrillic source a/b identities. The first is intended for liquids
lighter than water; the second for heavier liquids. No other numerical
scale readings or actual measured densities are provided.

For a fixed instrument mass, static flotation gives V_sub=m/rho_liquid.
It sinks farther into a less dense liquid, rises in a denser liquid; along
the stem density readings increase downward. Calibrate a fixed-mass upright
instrument using known reference liquids, temperature and same-g conditions;
water1000 identifies the source reference, not every liquid's density.

Make two suitable test-tube or lead-weighted wooden-stick instruments with
sealed/dry bodies and low ballast keeping the center of mass sufficiently
low for upright flotation. Adjust separately to retain a visible readable
stem over the lighter-than-water and heavier-than-water ranges. Mark water
reference and, using supplied known-density reference liquids, calibrate
further scale positions; no arbitrary evenly spaced numerical density scale
is justified for a changing cross-section. Keep the instrument clear of
walls/bottom, allow equilibrium, and read the liquid surface consistently.
Describe construction, calibration and measurements to be performed rather
than inventing successfully built instruments, tick values or data.
