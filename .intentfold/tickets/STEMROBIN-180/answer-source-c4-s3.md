## Common Raw Hydraulic Relation

For the original ideal hydraulic model, use the applied pressure communicated through the liquid:

\[
p=\frac{F_1}{S_1},\qquad F_2=pS_2,\qquad
\frac{F_2}{F_1}=\frac{S_2}{S_1}.
\]

The gain compares forces on the pistons, not a handle force or the load's mass. Use the stated no-friction assumptions where present. The textbook calculation introduces no hydrostatic height correction, piston-weight correction or extra lever ratio; none is invented here. This treatment of the communicated applied pressure does not assert that all absolute hydrostatic pressures at different heights are identical.

Areas can be kept in the same square-centimetre unit when forming their ratio. To compute a pressure in Pa, convert area to square metres using \(1\ {\rm cm}^2=10^{-4}\ {\rm m}^2\). Gain is dimensionless.

## Task4: Hydraulic-Brake Operation

Identity: exercise `q1`, source number `null`, group `g2`, printed group Assignment. Source reference `p0124#4`. This is the fourth task, not another numbered Exercise22 problem. Actual task figure103.

The original labels are1 pedal,2 master pressure cylinder with piston,3 brake pressure cylinder,4 brake blocks,5 drum,6 spring; the cylinders and connecting line contain special liquid.

**Application sequence:**

1. Press pedal1. Its linkage moves the piston of master cylinder2 and increases the liquid pressure.
2. The increased pressure is communicated through the liquid-filled connecting line to brake cylinder3, following the hydraulic principle.
3. The opposed wheel-cylinder pistons push the brake blocks4 outward against the inner surface of drum5.
4. The contact creates friction between the blocks and the rotating drum. This friction opposes the drum/wheel rotation, slowing the vehicle and, with sufficient braking, bringing it to rest.

The hydraulic action supplies the normal contact force. It is the block/drum friction, not the fluid directly rubbing the drum or an unsupported equality of all component forces, that produces the braking torque.

**Release:** after the pedal is released, the hydraulic loading falls. Spring6 pulls the blocks away from the drum; the wheel-cylinder pistons return and displaced liquid can return toward the master cylinder. The brake is released rather than remaining clamped merely because the line remains full of liquid.

**Answer:** pedal-driven master-cylinder pressure is transmitted through the special liquid to the wheel cylinder, whose pistons press the brake blocks against the drum; friction retards rotation. The spring withdraws the blocks on release.
