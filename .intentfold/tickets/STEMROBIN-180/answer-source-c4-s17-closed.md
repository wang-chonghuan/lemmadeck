## Four Questions

1. Suspend the empty small bucket and equal-volume cylinder from the spring,
   mark its extension, fully immerse the cylinder in a filled overflow
   vessel without bottom or wall support and collect all displaced liquid.
   The spring shortens. Transfer the whole displaced liquid into the hanging
   bucket while the cylinder remains immersed: the initial extension returns.
   The displaced liquid weight equals the upward buoyant force. A calibrated
   force-meter difference is a separate measuring method; the original165
   is not a numerical scale.
2. Buoyancy equals the weight of displaced liquid, not the object's weight
   in general and not the weight of all liquid in the container.
3. It is also called Archimedes' force. The named person is source-bound,
   not an invented modern identity.
4. F_A = rho_fluid*g*V_displaced; for complete immersion the displaced
   volume is the object's whole volume. It acts upward. P1=P-F_A describes
   supported/apparent weight, not loss of the object's mass.

## Five Practice Tasks

- g2-1: Equal masses of lead and aluminum give a greater aluminum volume.
  With equal arms, both cylinders fully immersed in the same fluid, the
  aluminum side gets greater buoyancy and rises; the lead side goes down.
  The direction is unchanged in alcohol, though the force difference is
  smaller. At fixed fluid density buoyancy is proportional to displaced
  volume. Check with an initially balanced beam, equal full immersion and
  no container contacts; record the predicted direction as an expectation,
  not as an experiment actually performed. Lead/alcohol handling is not
  safety advice and should be teacher-managed or theoretical in modern text.
- g2-2: Equal-volume aluminum cylinders have equal initial weights. The
  water side gets greater buoyancy than the alcohol side and rises when both
  are fully immersed. F_A is proportional to fluid density at fixed displaced
  volume. A controlled balanced-beam comparison tests it without fabricating
  measured values. The actual source table gives water1000 and alcohol800kg/m3.
- g2-3: Equal-volume iron/aluminum cylinders lose the same supported weight
  in water, so the beam stays balanced with its initial additional weights.
  The added balancing weights must remain outside the water; if immersed,
  their additional buoyancy is a different unspecified experiment.
- g2-4: 0.1dm3=0.0001m3. With water1000kg/m3 and g9.8N/kg,
  F_A=0.98N. The actual source liquid table gives kerosene800kg/m3:
  F_A=0.784N. If the allowed g approximately10 convention is used,
  the corresponding values are1N and0.8N. The iron density is irrelevant
  to buoyancy at this specified fully immersed volume.
- g2-5: Dense lead ballast adds substantial downward weight for a relatively
  small displaced volume and therefore counters the upward buoyancy of the
  diver/suit, helping maintain the intended submerged position. It does not
  reduce gravitational acceleration or remove buoyancy. This is the historical
  physical explanation, not modern diving, equipment or ballast advice.
