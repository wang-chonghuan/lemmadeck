## Essential Findings

### D1 [P1]: The Second Law Does Not Identify The Net Vector Force

Target: block 7, `p0141#5`; explanatory consequences in blocks 1, 2, 6, 10, 11 and 13.

The bold statement says `作用在物体上的力，与力的种类无关，等于物体的质量和它的加速度的乘积。` It does not say the resultant of all forces on the chosen body, unlike the first-law statement's explicit `合力`. A reader can therefore apply the equation separately to gravity, support or friction as if each were the entire mass times the body's actual acceleration.

Reviewer counterexample: a book at rest on a table has nonzero gravity and nonzero support, but zero acceleration. Neither individual force equals mass times that zero acceleration; their vector sum does. This is an error of physical meaning, not a cultural token or a request to add a formula absent from the source.

Essential teaching conditions: the chosen body's net external force, acceleration in the inertial frame already introduced, and the constant-mass scope of the familiar mass-times-acceleration form. Force and acceleration have direction as well as magnitude; acceleration has the net force's direction, not necessarily the velocity's direction. The opening's `力的量值` must not leave magnitude as a complete description of force. Mass correctly measures inertia, but block 2's `加速度决定于物体的固有属性` must be understood with the acting net force fixed, not as mass alone determining acceleration. No acceleration-ratio claim is inferred for arbitrary bodies with additional external forces.

Basis: P1, first/second-law sections; P2, sections 9-1 through 9-4. The later mention of mass in block 11 prevents a separate finding that the whole lesson forgot mass, but does not cure block 7's wrong force referent.

### D2 [P1]: Inertial Frames Do Not Make Newtonian Mechanics Universal

Target: block 10, `p0141#8`, especially `牛顿定律在原则上可以解决力学的任何问题`; scope also affects blocks 3, 11, 12 and 13.

Choosing an inertial frame does not make Newtonian predictions valid for every physical regime. The unqualified claim includes motion requiring relativistic or quantum descriptions. `在原则上` addresses ideal solvability, not the missing applicability boundary. The following force-to-acceleration-to-position discussion is useful and must be retained, but restricted to an applicable classical model with specified interactions, mass and adequate initial data.

Even within classical mechanics, these three laws do not independently supply a gravity, elastic or other force law. Knowing the current force alone is not knowing its future dependence on position, velocity, time or other interacting bodies. Likewise, position and velocity of a modeled point/center of mass do not specify every rotational or deformational degree of freedom of an arbitrary extended body.

The necessary boundary is a clear applicable Newtonian motion model, not a new advanced course: do not promise all mechanics from these laws and one translational initial state. A changing acceleration is allowed; constant acceleration is not required by Newton's laws. No relativistic formula, numerical speed cutoff, force-law replacement or new exercise is requested or authorized here.

Basis: P2, sections 9-4 through 9-7, for force models and initial data; P3, section 2-3, for the atomic/quantum limit; P4, section 16-1, for Newtonian laws' limited velocity range. The correct inertial-frame restriction in blocks 3-5 is retained, not called a source error.

### D3 [P2]: The Third-Law Pair Needs Explicit Different-Body Teaching

Target: blocks 8-9, `p0141#6/#7`.

The preserved law's core is correct in the stated Newtonian setting: the two bodies exert forces of the same interaction type with equal magnitudes and opposite directions. `彼此` suggests reciprocity, so this is not a finding that the printed equality itself is false. For this independent young learner, however, the statement needs the essential distinction between a reciprocal pair and two forces balancing on one body.

The two reciprocal forces act on different bodies. They cannot be canceled in a one-body force sum to conclude that interaction produces no acceleration. Equal reciprocal forces also do not imply equal accelerations when the bodies' masses differ. The resting book's gravity and table support are not one third-law pair merely because they balance; each has its own reciprocal partner on another body.

This is a teaching-completeness defect, distinct from changing the force type or weakening the equality. Keep mutual interaction, same type, equal magnitude and opposite direction; do not add claims that unequal masses exert unequal reciprocal forces or that action occurs first and reaction later.

Basis: P1, third-law section, which explicitly distinguishes A-on-B from B-on-A; P2, section 9-1, for the force/mass dependence of acceleration. The non-cancellation consequence is the reviewer's application of those statements to one-body accounting.

### D4 [P2]: The Spacecraft Example Confuses Modeled Prediction With Exact Foreknowledge

Target: block 12, `p0141#10 + p0142#1`.

The example asserts that the scientists know the force at every trajectory point and therefore know the spacecraft's position at any time. As an idealized calculation with known force models and initial data this illustrates the preceding chain well. As unqualified factual teaching about actual flight, it conflates a predicted trajectory with exact knowledge of all future forces and positions. Actual inference from measured inputs has limits; the candidate does not identify its idealization.

The launch example also follows an unqualified constant-mass second law without distinguishing a fixed-mass/coasting approximation from propelled flight with expelled propellant. If the latter is intended, mass evolution and exhaust momentum/thrust must be accounted for in a consistent model; merely differentiating the momentum of the remaining open spacecraft and calling it the external force is not a valid general repair.

Finally, force varying in time does not by itself imply that calculation requires a computer. Reviewer counterexample: a fixed-mass body with a known linear-in-time net force in one dimension can be integrated analytically with its initial data. Computers are practically appropriate for sufficiently complicated trajectory models, not logically required by every variable force.

Essential disposition: retain the spacecraft application and numerical-computation point, while distinguishing idealized/approximate prediction, the chosen mass model and practical computational complexity. No mission-specific facts, replacement dates, masses, spacecraft names or invented source wording are supplied.

Basis: P1's constant-mass distinction; P2, sections 9-5 through 9-7, for modeled integration, numerical approximation and computer use. The counterexample and model-consistency requirements are reviewer reasoning, not quotations from NASA or Caltech.
