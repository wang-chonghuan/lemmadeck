## c4-s5: Two Tasks

1. With unchanged membrane apparatus/liquid, greater depth yields greater
pressure difference and larger bulge. Compare bottom and side orientations
at equal depth; immerse with equal inside/outside surfaces as111/112 show,
so the same-liquid pressures balance and membranes flatten in the ideal
thin-membrane model. The removable bottom113 independently demonstrates
upward pressure. Explain observation→pressure, not merely name Pascal.
Different depths change pressure; at one point static-fluid pressure is
isotropic. Surface boundary pressures must be the same. Use stable liquid,
comparable membrane tension/apparatus; no invented performed readings.
2. Gas density is small in ordinary containers, so its gravitational pressure
variation rho*g*h is usually small relative to relevant pressure scale.
Neglecting its weight's contribution is not neglecting all gas pressure,
not zero gas weight, and not valid automatically over atmospheric heights.

## c4-s6: Ten Tasks

1. Gauge hydrostatic bottom pressure rho*g*h depends on density, depth and
local g; not bottom area/shape at fixed liquid and depth. Absolute pressure
also includes the imposed free-surface pressure.
2. At fixed rho/g and boundary pressure, hydrostatic contribution is directly
proportional to depth h.
3. At fixed h/g, hydrostatic contribution is directly proportional to rho.
4. For wall pressure at a specified point need rho, its vertical depth below
surface and g; include surface pressure if absolute is requested. One
bottom-depth value does not give uniform pressure over a tall wall.
5. p=rho*g*h for the gauge contribution, rho in kg/m^3, h metres,
g9.8N/kg gives pascals. Force on finite surface is not just p without area.
g2-1. At0.6m, table densities and g9.8 yield water5880Pa,

so S=F/p=0.005m^2=50cm^2. g cancels. The1m head is between

g3-1. Higher internal water pressure than outside drives flow from holes.
Near a vertical wall jets emerge horizontally, normal to wall, showing side
pressure. After exit gravity accelerates falling water, so paths bend.
Greater depth increases exit speed; horizontal range also depends on fall
height to the receiving surface. For ideal free jet from hole height z over

not unconditionally monotonic with depth. The source's drawn ordering is
qualitative apparatus geometry, not a universal deepest-always-farthest law.
Retain making three holes, releasing/observing all jets and all five questions;
actual records/experimental drawing are ungraded.
g3-2. With sealed shared headspace raised by delta p, tube bottoms all
remain submerged, tops open to same external pressure and same liquid:
delta p=rho*g*delta h. All three free surfaces reach the same height,
regardless of bends or differing inlet depths. At inlet depth d, reservoir
pressure is p_air+rho*g*d; outlet column pressure includes that same d,
which cancels. Demonstrates Pascal transmission plus hydrostatic balance.
Keep all three water tubes/fourth gas tube, apparatus construction/experiment,
repeat with different inlet depths and full explanation. Do not describe
pressurized gas as passing only through deepest water inlet. No invented

## c4-s7: Nine Tasks

1. In same liquid at rest, same surface pressure, flat horizontal bases of
area S and same depth h: each bottom p=rho*g*h, hence F=pS,
independent of container shape. Shape-independent force requires fixed S
as well as h; pressure alone does not require equal S.
2. F=rho*g*h*S, weight of imaginary vertical prism above the base,
not necessarily actual contained liquid's weight.
3. Exchange equal-base membrane vessels on the same119instrument,
fill to equal depths with same liquid and compare deflection; equal pointer
reading supports equal bottom load. Do not assign invented numerical ticks.
4. Different volumes/masses give equal bottom loads, contradicting naive
belief that all fluid weight must act only on bottom; vessel walls also exert
vertical forces. No genuine inconsistency or conservation-law violation.
5.120central kmnl column establishes rho*g*h at mn; isotropic transmission
gives same depth pressure to am/nb. Entire ab load equals hypothetical
abdc prism weight. Wall reactions explain the difference from actual mg.

small added water raises head and pressure sufficiently to split walls.
