### force-experiments: Complete True Set {a, b, d}

- a: TRUE. Removing the source magnet while keeping current removes the
  source magnetic-force cause of the original deflection. Disappearance of
  that deflection in the controlled comparison supports the causal claim.
  This is the ordinary before/after response, not an assertion of
  instantaneous return or vanishing of every possible ambient field.
- b: TRUE. With field and geometry fixed, reversing current reverses the
  magnetic force, as independently established from SOURCE block 7.
- c: FALSE. Current reversal gives `-F`; reversing the field as well gives
  `-(-F) = F`. Both changes together retain the original nonzero force
  direction, rather than reversing it. The stem permits all changes
  explicitly named in an option, so the joint change is not excluded by the
  comparison instruction.
- d: TRUE. With current direction fixed, interchanging poles reverses the
  field and magnetic-force direction, independently established from block 8.
- e: FALSE. The selected definition explicitly gives zero magnetic force
  when the conductor is parallel to the field. Current alone does not make
  that force nonzero.

### rotating-coil-model: Complete True Set {b}

- a: FALSE. Two complete rings may provide supply contact, but each brush
  remains connected to its own ring and winding end through rotation. A
  half-turn does not automatically exchange those end connections. The
  proposed commutation mechanism is absent; unchanged-current motion after
  equilibrium is restoring rather than continually driven in one sense.
- b: TRUE at the source's idealized model level. Insulated halves co-rotate
  with the frame, each winding end connects to one half, and brush contact
  exchange reverses winding current at the appropriate repeated half-turn
  phase after inertial passage. With the frame supplied, this sustains the
  original rotational sense. The independently derived first equilibrium
  plane is perpendicular to the field, although the visible option fails to
  elicit that result and therefore still participates in F1.
- c: FALSE. At exact equilibrium the axial turning moment is zero.
  Reversing current reverses the moment's sign, so zero remains zero at that
  same orientation. A perfectly stationary ideal frame supplies a
  counterexample to the claimed inevitable self-start. Perturbations can
  change an unstable equilibrium, but do not guarantee the previous
  rotational sense. Continued supply alone does not prove either guarantee.
- d: FALSE. Electrically connecting the halves joins both winding ends and,
  through the brushes, the opposite source terminals. That shorts the
  intended split-ring circuit rather than preserving isolated alternating
  end connections. It cannot realize the stated powered commutation model.
