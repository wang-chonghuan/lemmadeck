## 1 / g1 / Governing Law

Pascal's law: pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted throughout
the fluid. Under the lesson's equilibrium model it supplies the common
additional pressure beneath the pistons. Do not replace this with equal
forces on unequal piston areas. This is a naming/explanation task; ungraded
equivalent correct wording is sufficient.

## 2 / g1 / Ideal Force Gain

Ignoring piston friction and other departures from the stated elementary
model, F1/S1 = F2/S2, so F2/F1 = S2/S1. The larger piston provides a force
gain equal to its area divided by the small piston's area, not the ratio of
diameters or an equal force. This is an ideal equilibrium statement; a
height-induced hydrostatic difference, piston weights or losses must not be
silently mixed into a different model.

The lesson's example uses 10 and 100 square centimetres, giving gain 10
and F2 = 100 N for F1 = 10 N. These are worked-example data, not task 2's
newly supplied measurements or a universal hydraulic-machine gain.

## g2-1 / Distance Tradeoff

For incompressible liquid with no leakage, the displaced volumes agree:
S1 d1 = S2 d2. Therefore d1/d2 = S2/S1. The input small piston must move
farther than the output large piston in exactly the ideal force-gain ratio.
The gained force is paid for by a greater input distance, not by free work
or energy creation. Combining the two relations gives F1 d1 = F2 d2 in
the ideal lossless model. Real friction increases the required input work.
No stroke length or work value is supplied, so none is fabricated.

## g2-2 / Using Air

Yes, a pressure-driven analogous machine can use confined compressed air:
gas pressure can act on pistons and communicate through a connected system.
This is a pneumatic machine, not proof that air is incompressible.

Air's compressibility changes the stroke response: input motion first can
compress gas and store energy; the immediate equal-displaced-liquid-volume
relation above cannot simply be carried over. Response can be elastic or
delayed, and accurate stiff force/stroke transfer differs from hydraulics.
At static equilibrium with appropriate ideal assumptions the piston force
is still pressure times area. Do not say all air machines are impossible,
that gas pressure does not transmit, or that arbitrary pneumatic machines
have exactly the same displacement relation as incompressible hydraulics.
This is a full explanation task, ungraded.
