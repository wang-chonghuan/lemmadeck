## Independently Solved Written Tasks

16. Through one point or two distinct points: infinitely many planes.
Through three distinct points: one if noncollinear, infinitely many if
collinear. Through all four points with no collinear triple: one if coplanar,
none otherwise. Not the count of planes through each chosen triple.

17. A belongs to alpha; b need not lie in alpha, since it can meet alpha
only at A.

18. Four noncoplanar points cannot contain a collinear triple: that line
and the fourth point would fit a plane. Converse false, e.g. four rectangle
vertices.

19. Any three distinct noncollinear points admit a circumcircle in their
plane. The stated exclusion therefore forces collinearity and hence
coplanarity, not a noncoplanar tetrahedron.

20. Distinct parallel lines determine one plane. A transversal meeting both
has two distinct points in that plane and lies in it.

21. False: a line through their common point but outside their plane meets
both given lines at that same point.

22. Exactly one and exactly two common points are possible. Exactly three
are not: a plane then contains two distinct points of one ray and hence
infinitely many points of that ray.

23. Every pair of rays determines one plane and no two such planes coincide
under the no-three-coplanar condition: 3, 6, n(n-1)/2.

24. A common point need not imply a common line. Pairwise intersection lines
can be one common line or three distinct concurrent lines, never exactly two.

25. Intersecting diagonals determine a containing plane; if the diagonal
lines coincide, every vertex is collinear and a containing plane still
exists. Pull strings between opposite table feet and check contact where
their diagonals cross; the task is a coplanarity model, not a guarantee on
uneven terrain.

26. Opposite closed half-spaces with boundary alpha have intersection alpha
and union the whole space. If two closed half-spaces intersect exactly in
the plane alpha, their boundaries coincide and they are opposite. Union
equal intersection forces equality of the two sets, coincident boundaries
and the same side.
