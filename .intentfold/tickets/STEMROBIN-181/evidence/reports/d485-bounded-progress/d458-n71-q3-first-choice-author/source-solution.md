## Independent Mathematical Solution

The learner must identify incident rays whose reflected paths can be constructed
without solving a fresh incidence geometry at an arbitrary surface point.
The written task is a collective selection/listing task, not four separate
binary tasks and not a numeric computation. All four convenient types matter.

1. A ray through the center of curvature reaches the spherical surface along
   its local normal, which passes through that center. Normal incidence makes
   the reflected ray retrace the same straight path with reversed direction.
   This is the center-of-curvature rule, not the focus rule.
2. A ray through the focus is reflected parallel to the principal axis in the
   supplied paraxial concave-mirror model. It is the reverse path of the
   axis-parallel incident ray that reflects through the focus. It need not
   retrace its own incoming straight line.
3. A ray reaching the pole reflects with the principal axis as the normal there.
   Equality of incidence and reflection angles makes the incident/reflected
   ray directions symmetric about that axis. Reaching the pole does not mean
   the incident ray itself lies along the axis or through the curvature center.
4. A ray initially parallel to the principal axis is reflected through the
   focus in the supplied paraxial model. It is not generally parallel to that
   axis after reflection. Together with the reverse-focus rule, this supplies
   two conveniently related but distinct incident-ray constructions.

From the same object point, draw two available distinct convenient rays and
apply their reflection rules. In the real-image situation used by the supplied
model, the intersection of the reflected paths locates the image point. The
other modeled reflected rays pass through that same point. Two distinct lines
with a unique intersection determine the location; a third ray can check it but
is not required. A single reflected path leaves a locus of possible points and
does not by itself provide the usual two-ray location construction.

"Any two" is not an unconditional assertion about arbitrary lines: the rays
must start from the same object point, belong to the stated model, actually be
reflected by the mirror, and give distinct paths that locate the image uniquely.
If chosen paths coincide, they provide only one independent line. The source's
any-two rule presumes the ordinary nondegenerate construction.

A central hole is not a reflecting surface. An incident ray reaching it has
no reflected ray supplied by the pole rule. If the other convenient rays reach
reflecting portions, select two of those instead; the existence of the hole
does not require abandoning imaging or requiring all four convenient rays.
Do not substitute a fictitious reflection at the hole for an available ray.
