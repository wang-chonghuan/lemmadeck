[0] Gas laws describe how a gas's pressure, volume, and temperature are connected.
Changing one of these state variables affects the others, so a single reading does not fully describe the gas's state.
[1] Thermodynamics also uses relationships between state variables to measure temperature, as the original book explains in § $11$.
For gases, the relationships between pressure $p$, volume $V$, and temperature $t$ have a relatively simple form.

[2] If pressure, volume, and temperature all change at once, it is hard to find a clear pattern in an experiment.
A simpler approach is to study a fixed amount of gas, keeping one of $p$, $V$, and $t$ constant while comparing the other two.
A quantitative relationship between two of these variables, while the third stays constant, is called a gas law.
