# 44. Sound Resonance

Sounding objects can vibrate freely.
An external periodic force can drive them.
If the force's frequency is near their natural frequency, resonance can greatly boost the response.

Two identical tuning forks demonstrate **sound resonance**.
Set them a short distance apart on wooden resonance boxes.
Let the box openings face each other (Figure 83).

Tap one arm of one fork with a small hammer.
The other fork soon begins to sound as well.
Sound waves from the first apply a periodic force to it.

Their matching natural frequencies allow a large response.
The response depends on the frequency difference.
Outside the receiving fork's narrow resonance band, it is much weaker.

Attach a light metal clip to either identical fork.
This shifts its natural frequency and can detune the pair.
With enough detuning, the response may become inaudible.
