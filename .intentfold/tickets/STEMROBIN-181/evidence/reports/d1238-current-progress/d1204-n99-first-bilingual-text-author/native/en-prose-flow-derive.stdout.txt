[0] Newtonian mechanics works well when a body's speed is much smaller than the speed of light.
Classical ideas of space and time are then good approximations.
[1] Newton's second law takes the form:
$m\frac{\overrightarrow{\Delta v}}{\Delta t}=\vec{F}$ (8.7)

[2] At low speeds, the law keeps the same form when we change from one inertial frame to another.
Near light speed, this classical constant-mass form is no longer sufficient.

[3] If we used the classical constant-mass model in (8.7) indefinitely, a constant force would increase speed without limit.
But the speed of light in vacuum is the limiting speed, which a body with rest mass cannot exceed.

[4] Newton also expressed his second law through the rate of change of momentum:
$\frac{\overrightarrow{\Delta p}}{\Delta t}=\vec{F}$ (8.8)
This form is the starting point for describing high-speed motion.

[5] The momentum in this notation is $\vec{p}=m\vec{v}$.
