[0] The new law of velocity addition agrees with the relativistic concepts of space and time.
The classical law cannot be generally valid because it conflicts with the constancy of the speed of light in vacuum.
[1] Suppose a train moves at speed $v$ and a light wave travels inside the carriage in the direction of the train's motion.
The speed of the light wave relative to Earth must still be $c$, **not** $v+c$.
The new law of velocity addition must also give this result.

[3] Let $v_1$ denote the object's velocity relative to $K_1$, and let $v_2$ denote the same object's velocity relative to $K$.
The relativistic law of velocity addition gives:
$v_2=\frac{v_1+v}{1+\frac{v_1v}{c^2}}$ (8.6)

[6] A notable property of the relativistic law is that, for the allowed speeds $v_1$ and $v$ (neither greater than $c$), the resulting speed $v_2$ does not exceed $c$.

$v_2=\frac{2c}{2}=c$.
