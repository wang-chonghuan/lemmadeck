[17] Fiber optics uses total internal reflection to carry light and images along bundles of transparent, flexible fibers. Such an arrangement is a light guide.
A glass light guide consists of cylindrical glass fibers. In the usual core-and-cladding structure, each fiber has transparent cladding with a refractive index lower than that of its glass core.
Repeated total internal reflections let light follow a straight or curved fiber. Bends must still preserve the interface and incidence-angle conditions needed for guidance; an arbitrarily sharp bend need not keep the light in the fiber.

[18] Many such fiber bundles can be gathered into a ribbon-like light guide.
When an image is transmitted, each fiber carries the light corresponding to one image element (Figure 159). To reproduce the image at the output, the relative arrangement of the fibers must correspond at both ends.
These fiber-bundle light guides can be used in medicine to observe and examine internal organs. Sending light through a guide avoids the unwanted local heating from placing an ordinary light bulb directly at the observation site.
