第二章　电振荡
17. 自由电振荡和受迫电振荡

BLOCK 0 [p] ["p0048#3"]
电振荡的发现带有一些偶然性。
当时，人们发明了莱顿瓶。
这是最早的电容器，用来储存电荷。

人们学会了用静电起电机给莱顿瓶加上大量电荷。
这种机器能产生静电。
接着，人们开始观察莱顿瓶怎样放电。

用线圈连接莱顿瓶的两块极板，放电的电流就会经过线圈。
线圈里的铁芯会被磁化，也就是变得像磁铁一样。
电流能使铁芯磁化，这本来并不奇怪。

奇怪的是，人们无法预先判断铁芯哪端会成为北极，哪端会成为南极。
即使在大致相同的条件下反复实验，得到的磁极方向也可能不同。
铁芯可以朝相反的方向被磁化。

人们经过很大努力，才明白原因。
电容器通过线圈放电时，会产生振荡。
在这段放电时间里，电容器会多次反复充电，电流也会多次改变方向。

电流方向会变，所以铁芯被磁化的方向也可能不同。

BLOCK 1 [p] ["p0048#4"]
**电荷、电流强度和电压发生周期性或近似周期性的变化，叫做电振荡。**
周期性变化，就是同样的变化过程按一定时间间隔反复出现。
近似周期性变化，就是每次变化大致相似，但不完全一样。

电荷表示带电的量。
电流强度表示电流的大小。
电压表示电路中两点之间的电势差。

BLOCK 2 [p] ["p0048#5"]
让电路产生电振荡并不难。
这差不多像把物体挂在弹簧上，让它振动一样简单。
可是，观察电振荡就没那么容易了。

我们不能直接看见电容器反复充电，也不能直接看见线圈里的电流。
而且，电振荡通常频率很高。
这表示它在很短的时间里就会反复变化很多次。

BLOCK 3 [p] ["p0048#6"]
电子示波器很适合用来观察和分析电振荡。
它能把看不见的电压变化变成屏幕上的曲线。

BLOCK 4 [p] ["p0049#1"]
这里介绍的是使用显像管的电子示波器。
显像管里有一束细细的电子束，射向荧光屏。
电子打到荧光屏上时，屏幕会发光。

给水平偏转板加上“锯齿形”的交变扫描电压$u_p$（图24）。
这个符号表示用来让电子束横向扫过屏幕的电压。
这种电压比较缓慢地增大，然后突然减小。

偏转板之间的电场使电子束在水平方向匀速扫过屏幕。
然后，电子束几乎一瞬间就返回起点。
这个过程会反复进行。

再把竖直偏转板连接到电容器上。
电容器放电时，电压的振荡会使电子束上下振荡。
横向扫描和上下振荡合在一起，就画出了电压随时间变化的“展开图”（图25）。

这条曲线与带沙漏的摆在移动纸片上留下的曲线很相似。
振荡会随时间衰减，也就是振荡的幅度越来越小①。

电子束照过以后，这种屏幕还能发光较长时间。

BLOCK 11 [p] ["p0049#7"]
这种振荡叫做**自由电振荡**。
先给电容器充电，系统就离开了平衡状态。
此后，不需要外界反复推动，系统自身就能发生振荡。

这很像先把摆拉离平衡位置，再让它自己摆动。
给电容器充电，就相当于把摆拉开。

BLOCK 12 [p] ["p0049#8","p0050#1"]
电路里也能产生**受迫电振荡**。
这时，外界向电路提供周期性变化的电动势，反复推动电荷运动。
电动势表示电源给单位电荷提供的能量，不是力。

例如，让绕有几匝导线的线圈框在匀强磁场里转动，就能产生交变电动势（图26）。
一匝就是导线绕成的一圈。
匀强磁场表示磁场的大小和方向处处相同。

线圈框转动时，穿过它的磁通量会周期性变化。
磁通量表示磁场穿过线圈所围面积的情况。
根据电磁感应定律，感生电动势也会周期性变化。

在这里，电子随线圈框一起运动，磁场对这些运动的电子施加力。
这个力使电子沿导线移动。
因此，转动的线圈框能给电路提供交变电动势。

电路闭合时，交变电流就会通过电流计。
电流计用指针显示电流的变化。
电流方向反复改变，指针就在平衡位置附近来回摆动。

Chapter 2: Electrical Oscillations
17. Free and Forced Electrical Oscillations

BLOCK 0 [p] ["p0048#3"]
Electrical oscillations were discovered partly by chance.
At that time, people had invented the Leyden jar.
It was one of the earliest capacitors, used to store electric charge.

People learned to put a large amount of charge on a Leyden jar using an electrostatic generator.
This machine produces static electricity.
Then people began to observe how the jar discharged.

When a coil connected the jar's two plates, the discharge current flowed through the coil.
The iron core inside the coil became magnetized, meaning that it acted like a magnet.
There was nothing strange about this: a current can magnetize an iron core.

The strange part was that people could not predict which end would become the north pole and which the south pole.
Even when they repeated the experiment under nearly the same conditions, the poles could point the other way.
The iron core could be magnetized in opposite directions.

It took a great deal of effort to understand the reason.
Oscillations occur when a capacitor discharges through a coil.
During this discharge, the capacitor can charge again many times, and the current can reverse direction many times.

Since the current changes direction, the direction in which the iron core is magnetized can also differ.

BLOCK 1 [p] ["p0048#4"]
**Periodic or nearly periodic changes in electric charge, current, and voltage are called electrical oscillations.**
A periodic change is a pattern that repeats at regular time intervals.
A nearly periodic change repeats in much the same way, but not exactly.

Electric charge describes how much charge is present.
Current describes the size of the flow of charge.
Voltage is the electric potential difference between two points in a circuit.

BLOCK 2 [p] ["p0048#5"]
It is not hard to make electrical oscillations occur in a circuit.
It is about as simple as hanging an object from a spring and making it vibrate.
But observing electrical oscillations is not so easy.

We cannot directly see a capacitor charging again and again, or the current inside a coil.
Electrical oscillations also usually have a high frequency.
This means that the changes repeat many times in a very short time.

BLOCK 3 [p] ["p0048#6"]
An electronic oscilloscope is well suited to observing and studying electrical oscillations.
It turns invisible changes in voltage into a curve on a screen.

BLOCK 4 [p] ["p0049#1"]
The oscilloscope described here uses a cathode-ray tube.
Inside the tube, a thin beam of electrons travels toward a fluorescent screen.
The screen glows where the electrons hit it.

A sawtooth-shaped alternating sweep voltage $u_p$ is applied to the horizontal deflection plates (Figure 24).
This symbol means the voltage used to sweep the electron beam across the screen.
The voltage increases relatively slowly, then drops suddenly.

The electric field between the plates makes the beam sweep horizontally across the screen at a steady speed.
The beam then returns to its starting point almost instantly.
This process repeats.

Next, the vertical deflection plates are connected to the capacitor.
As the capacitor discharges, its oscillating voltage makes the beam move up and down.
The horizontal sweep and the up-and-down motion together draw a trace of voltage over time (Figure 25).

This curve is much like the curve left on moving paper by a pendulum carrying an hourglass.
The oscillations decay with time, meaning that their amplitude becomes smaller①.

BLOCK 5 [p] ["p0049#9"]
① The oscillations in this circuit decay within a fraction of a second (less than a second).
An oscilloscope with a special fluorescent screen makes this brief oscillation easier to see.
After the electron beam strikes it, this screen continues to glow for a longer time.

BLOCK 11 [p] ["p0049#7"]
These are called **free electrical oscillations**.
First, charging the capacitor moves the system away from equilibrium.
After that, the system can oscillate on its own, without repeated pushes from outside.

This is like pulling a pendulum away from its equilibrium position and letting it swing on its own.
Charging the capacitor is like pulling the pendulum aside.

BLOCK 12 [p] ["p0049#8","p0050#1"]
A circuit can also have **forced electrical oscillations**.
Here, an outside source supplies an electromotive force that changes periodically, repeatedly driving the charges.
Electromotive force, or EMF, means the energy a source provides per unit charge; it is not a force.

For example, a rotating frame with a coil of several turns produces an alternating EMF in a uniform magnetic field (Figure 26).
A turn is one loop of wire.
In a uniform magnetic field, the field has the same strength and direction everywhere.

As the coil frame turns, the magnetic flux through it changes periodically.
Magnetic flux describes how the magnetic field passes through the area enclosed by the coil.
According to the law of electromagnetic induction, the induced EMF also changes periodically.

Here, the electrons move along with the coil frame, and the magnetic field exerts a force on these moving electrons.
This force makes the electrons move along the wire.
The rotating coil frame can therefore supply an alternating EMF to the circuit.

When the circuit is closed, an alternating current flows through the galvanometer.
This instrument uses a pointer to show changes in current.
As the current repeatedly reverses direction, the pointer swings back and forth around its equilibrium position.
