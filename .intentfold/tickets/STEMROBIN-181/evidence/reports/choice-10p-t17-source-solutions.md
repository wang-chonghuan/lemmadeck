## 1. Spring Stiffness

For a fixed-support, negligible-mass Hooke-law spring with negligible resistance,
$m=100\ \mathrm{g}=0.100\ \mathrm{kg}$ and $\nu=2\ \mathrm{Hz}$ are the given
mass and ordinary frequency. The angular frequency is
$\omega=2\pi\nu=4\pi\ \mathrm{rad/s}$.
Thus $k=m\omega^2=1.6\pi^2\ \mathrm{N/m}\approx15.79\ \mathrm{N/m}$.
The task is to infer stiffness, not substitute the ordinary frequency as angular
frequency, and to convert grams before giving the stiffness unit.

## 3. Two Pendulum Lengths

The first pendulum makes $n_1=10$ complete oscillations while the second makes
$n_2=6$ in the same elapsed time and gravitational field.
$T_1/T_2=n_2/n_1=3/5$ and hence $l_1/l_2=9/25$.
The slower second pendulum is longer, so the given positive difference means
$l_2-l_1=16\ \mathrm{cm}$.

$(l_1,l_2)=(9\ \mathrm{cm},25\ \mathrm{cm})$.

## 5. First Arrival: Pendulum Versus Free Fall

Let $l$ be the light inextensible string length and $g$ the common uniform
gravitational acceleration. Both point balls start from rest simultaneously.
In the nonzero small-angle approximation the pendulum first reaches its lowest
position after a quarter period:
$t_{\mathrm{pend}}=(\pi/2)\sqrt{l/g}$.
The vertically falling ball covers the full support-to-equilibrium distance $l$,
so $t_{\mathrm{fall}}=\sqrt{2l/g}$.
Their ratio is $\pi/(2\sqrt2)\approx1.111>1$; the free-falling ball arrives first.
The comparison concerns arrival times, not the pendulum's small release height.
Air resistance and inter-ball interaction are excluded by the written model.

## 9. Sliding Block in the Spherical Bowl

## 10. Approximate Road-Induced Resonance

bumps and free vertical period $T_0=1.5\ \mathrm{s}$.

$f_{\mathrm{pass}}=u/d$. The free frequency is
$f_0=1/T_0=2/3\ \mathrm{Hz}$.

higher harmonics, the main near-resonance estimate is

This is not an exact peak-speed prediction for arbitrary damping or road shape.
