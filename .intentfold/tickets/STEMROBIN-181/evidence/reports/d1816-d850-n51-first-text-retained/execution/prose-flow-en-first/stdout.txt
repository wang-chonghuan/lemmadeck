[0] The connection between electric and magnetic fields shows that they are not unrelated fields.
In a given reference frame, they describe aspects of the same complete electromagnetic field.
[1] The original textbook uses the statements that a changing magnetic field is accompanied by an electric field, and vice versa, to emphasize their dynamical coupling.
More precisely, changing magnetic flux through a fixed surface is related to electric-field circulation along its boundary; electric current and changing electric flux through a fixed surface are related to magnetic-field circulation around its boundary.

[2] Circulation means adding the tangential contributions of a field along a closed path.
These laws link time dependence with spatial variation; they do not require both fields to be nonzero at every point, or describe one field disappearing before creating the other.

[9] Electric and magnetic fields are two kinds of components of the same complete **electromagnetic field** in a given reference frame.
The original textbook calls the electromagnetic field a special form of matter and emphasizes that its objective existence does not depend on human knowledge.
This is the textbook's philosophical description, not an additional experimental law.
